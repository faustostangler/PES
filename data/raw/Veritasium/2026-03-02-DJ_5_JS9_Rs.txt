Try this. Put on a latex glove and then put your hand into ice cold water. You'll notice it still feels wet. But that's strange because the glove is impermeable to water, but it still feels wet. That's because we actually don't have a way to directly measure wetness.

Instead, we use temperature changes as a proxy. And that works most of the time because water conducts heat 23 times faster than air. So your skin touches water, it pulls away heat, and your brain interprets that loss of heat as wet. And that's why 15° water feels a lot colder than 15° air. As long as it kept us dry enough to survive and to not die of hypothermia, this heat trick was good enough. But not so for insects. At

these scales, water is dangerously [music] sticky. So many insects adapted these microscopic hairs, which deform in the presence of moisture. This let them detect water [music] directly. So flies actually know something we don't, whether water really is wet.
