Your agent loop has five blocks: plan, edit, run, verify, fix, and four of them are basically solved. The AI does the work fine. Checking the work is the problem. It is the gap Addy Osmani, Peter Steinberger, and Boris Cherny all keep circling back to. Verification, the one block in the loop that is allowed to say no. So, let me show you what I

actually use for that block, Test Sprite, an open-source CLI, Apache 2.0. Here's what it actually does. You point it at your app, the live app, not mocks, and it runs real sessions against it, clicking, typing, exercising the flows a user would. When something breaks, it doesn't just go red.

It hands your agent a failure bundle, the exact step that failed, a screenshot of the moment, the root cause, and a suggested fix. Your agent reads that bundle, patches the code, and reruns the suite unattended. You are not in that loop. I queue work in the evening, and the plan, edit, run, verify cycle just keeps turning. Two details that sold me.

Every run is stored, so the history becomes permanent memory. Your agent knows what broke last Tuesday, even after its context window forgot. And it's genuinely open, Apache 2.0, the full CLI on GitHub, plus a public reference project, Coder Cup, where you can inspect real runs and real failure bundles before you install anything. It runs while I sleep, and I wake up to a loop that closed itself. Links in the
