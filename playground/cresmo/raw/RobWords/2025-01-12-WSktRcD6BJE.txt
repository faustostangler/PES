I’ve had a baby! Her name is Rosie and she is the best human being on the planet. Just ahead of her mum and despite her dad. And as such my life is currently a series of interludes between trips to the changing table. “It’s another pooh!”

This has given me plenty of time to ponder: what is it that I am slapping on little Rosie’s clean little arse? In my native Britain, we’d always call it a nappy. But for Americans and a load of other international Englishes, it’s called a diaper. Now I’m always minded to go with the British English. It’s more traditional, isn’t it?

Nappy is a cutesy shortening of the old term “baby’s napkin”. Napkin in turn means “small nappe”. A nappe is an Old French term for a tablecloth. So that makes the word really old. But what about diaper? Some new-fangled American invention, surely?

Well no. Diaper goes back at least as far as nappy. It’s from another Old French word meaning a white cloth from the mysterious east, from Byzantine Constantinople. And its usage was common in Britain for many centuries too. Look, here’s Shakespeare using it.

But it died out in the UK and now we think of diaper as an Americanism, eugh. Like pacifier, instead of dummy, which is not okay. But now I live in Germany and that means I feel I have a right to pick and choose which terms I use when I’m speaking English with my international English-speaking friends. The German word is Windel by the way, which is just adorable. So out of nappy and diaper, which one am I going to use?

Well, to be honest, I don’t think there’s anything between them. I’m going to use both. Let me know which word you use where you’re from in the comments.
