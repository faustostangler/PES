A lot of people think “cellar door” is the most beautiful sounding term in the English language. How can that possibly be? Well, it's a long story. And it’s one that starts in 1903, with the publication of a book called Gee-Boy, in which, during a discussion about words divorced from their meaning the narrator describes how "an Italian savant maintained that the most beautiful combination of English sounds was ‘cellar-door’.” This idea has then been kicked around by other authors ever since.

The likes of J.R.R. Tolkien and C.S. Lewis have both extolled the

mellifluous virtues of the cellar door. A little more recently, although still a quarter of a century ago, my God, it was mentioned in the film Donnie Darko, too. “…cellar door is the most beautiful.” And did you know that the production company originally behind Who Wants To Be A Millionaire was called Celador In a deliberate nod to the cosmic beauty of the phrase “cellar door”.
