A ladybug lands on the 12 of a clock, and every second she moves randomly to a neighboring number, either one step clockwise or one step counterclockwise. Here each time she touches a number I'm colouring it red, and on this run of the simulation, the last number to get coloured is 3. And if I run the simulation a second time, again where she continues until every number is coloured, the last one to get coloured is 1. My puzzle for you is what is the probability that the very last number to get coloured is the 6? This is the first in a set of monthly puzzles that I'll be posting this year as part of a collaboration with the mathematician Peter Winkler.

The spirit of it is to not give away the answer right away, we both want to give you a chance to mull it over. Every month Peter is going to hop onto a Zoom call to talk through a very elegant way to answer the relevant puzzle, and if you want to sign up to join those, go to momath.org slash mindbenders. It's all part of the MoMath Museum's Year of Math.
