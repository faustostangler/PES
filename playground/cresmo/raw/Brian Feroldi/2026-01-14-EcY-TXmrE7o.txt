Stock metrics every investor should know explained visually by Brian Froley. First is earnings per share or EPS. This is net income divided by total shares outstanding. This measures the profitability of the company on a per share basis. A general statement, the higher this number is the better. Next

is price to earnings ratio or P/E, which is the stock price divided by earnings per share. This tells you whether a stock is expensive or cheap. As a general statement, the lower this number is the better. Then there's return on equity, which is net income divided by shareholders equity. This tells you how effective the company is at turning equity into profit. As a general

guideline, you want this number to be 15% or higher. Next is debt to equity, which measures the company's total debt divided by the company's total equity. It shows you how much leverage is in the company. As a general statement, the lower this number is the better. Then there's the current ratio, which is current assets divided by current liabilities. This shows you how liquid

the company is. As a general rule, you want this number to be above one. Then there's gross margin, which is gross profit divided by revenue. This shows you the profitability of the product or service. As a general statement, the higher this number is the better. Before
