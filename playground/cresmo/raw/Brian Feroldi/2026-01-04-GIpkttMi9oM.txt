Have you ever heard of the rule of 72? This is a formula that everyone should memorize. You take 72 and you divide it by the asset's growth rate. What that will tell you is how many years it takes approximately for that asset value to double. For example, if a stock is growing at 6% per year, 72 divided by 6 is 12 years for that asset to double. 8%

it takes 9 years to double. At 12% it takes 6 years to double. This is a good mental trick in three specific cases. First is to figure out the time impact of investing. If you have $200,000 and it's expected to grow 10% per year, you can expect that 200k to double every 7.2

years. So it becomes 400k in 7 years, 800k in 14 years, and 1.6 million in 22 years. Second is a case of risk versus reward. If you're accepting a 3% return on asset, it'll take 24 years to double.

Whereas if you're expecting a 12% return on asset, it takes 6 years to double. The third shortcut is figuring out inflation. If inflation is 4% per year, your purchasing power will erode every 18 years or so. For example, if a movie ticket cost $8 in 2008, that same ticket will cost $16 by 26, meaning that your purchasing power has been cut in half.
