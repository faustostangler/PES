Yield explained simply by Brian Foli. A yield is an investment's return from income expressed as a percentage and typically the income that you get from the asset is either an interest or dividend payments. Yields are very useful with things like bonds, preferred stocks, dividend stocks, ETF, and mutual funds. The formula for yield is very simple. Take the income expected to earn of that asset over the course of year.

Divide that by the asset price. And it's important to note that the yield that you get is the return that you receive only from the income received in the stock versus the total return is the return that you get from the income and the price change in that asset. The company's yield can be actually very different number than the total return that you receive. It's a very useful tool for judging and comparing investments to the other and it's also useful for projecting future returns of the asset based on the income that you might receive.
