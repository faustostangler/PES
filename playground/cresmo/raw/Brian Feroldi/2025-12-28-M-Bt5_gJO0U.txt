Key takeaways from the Joys of Compounding by Gautam Baid, explained visually by Brian Feroldi. First, you want to compound wisdom and not just wealth. Knowledge builds like capital and grows exponentially over time. Two, always invest with a margin of safety. Avoid ruin and protect the downside first. Three, long-term thinking is your

edge. The idea is to hold great businesses through thick and thin and let compounding do the heavy lifting. Four, read voraciously. Reading is to the mind what exercise is to the body. Five, patience is a superpower. Doing

nothing is often the most profitable action. Six, study quality businesses. Focus on moat, reinvestment runways, and great management teams. Seven, keep mental models in mind. Invert, think probabilistically, and embrace second-order thinking. Eight, life is a

series of feedback loops. Good habits amplify and so do bad ones. Nine, character compounds, too. Reputation, trust, and discipline build or erode over time. And 10, stay calm under pressure. Your temperament is what beats

intelligence. If you're a fan of investing, this is a wonderful book to read. And if visual summaries like this are useful, you know what to do.
