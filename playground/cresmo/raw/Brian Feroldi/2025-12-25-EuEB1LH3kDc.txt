What's the difference between margin and markup? Explained visually by Brian Ferald. Margin shows the percentage that remains after you subtract all cost of goods sold. Formula is gross profit divided by revenue times 100. Markup shows how much more your selling price is than the amount the sale item cost you. Formula is gross profit divided by

cost of goods sold times 100. For example, let's say a bike shop sells a bike for $200 and buys that bike from its suppliers for $150. To find the margin, we would subtract the $200 selling price from the $150 cost, giving us $50 in gross profit. $50 in gross profit divided by the $200 selling price equals a 25% margin. Find the markup. We

would take the selling price and subtract the cost of the bike, giving us $50 in gross profit. We divide the $50 in gross profit by the $150 the bike cost us, giving us a 33% markup. If you're in business, it's incredibly important to know the difference between margin and markup. Visual explanations like this are useful. You know what to do.
