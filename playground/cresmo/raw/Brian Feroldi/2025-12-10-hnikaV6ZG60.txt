the historic volatility of returns by various holding periods explained visually by Brian Feraldi. If you study the returns of various asset classets over the last 75 years, you will see that stocks, bonds, and a 60/40 portfolio offer different levels of risk and return. The average annual return of a stockowned portfolio is about 11.6%, bond portfolio is about 5.2% and a 60/40 portfolio is about 9.4%. However, if you

look at rolling one-year returns, the numbers are all over the map. Stocks returned as much as 52% in a single year and minus 37% in a single year. The returns for bonds were more muted and the blended portfolio returns were also more modest. As you study those same returns over longer holding periods, what you see is that the average annualized returns start to bend towards their historic norms. My favorite visual in this entire chart is that over 20-year holding period, the average annualized return of all three asset classes was hugely positive. This shows

that the secret to making money in the market is always to just increase your holding period. [Music]
