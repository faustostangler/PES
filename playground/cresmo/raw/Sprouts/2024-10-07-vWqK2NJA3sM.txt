Some teens now identify as a gender different from the sex assigned to them at birth and about 3-10% enter their reproductive years with an interest in the same sex. During Childhood, boys tend to be more physically active, exhibiting greater interest in objects and exploration. Girls are less active, take a higher interest in people, and seem to form a better understanding of them. During Puberty, kids become teenagers. Their bodies change, and the differences between boys and girls become more obvious.

Around the ages of 8 to 13, girls start to get taller, their body shape begins to change, and hair grows in new places. They also have elevated levels of the hormone estrogen, are highly social, and tend to show more empathy. Boys enter puberty a bit later, with a growth spurt that peaks around age 14. They develop more muscle mass, body hair, and a larger Adam’s apple. Boys also produce more testosterone and develop a higher aggression potential. They are now larger, faster, significantly stronger, and

about 10x more likely to end up in jail.
