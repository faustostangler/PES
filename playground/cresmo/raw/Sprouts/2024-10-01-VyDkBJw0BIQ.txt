Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder, or ADHD, is mostly of genetic origin. Lisa, a young girl who wants to become a writer, will help us understand what it means to grow up with ADHD. During early childhood, Lisa has a tough time paying attention. She gets distracted. In middle school, Lisa starts doodling to help her stay focused.

During high school, her mind starts racing — with a thousand thoughts in her head. She now wears headphones, to isolate the noise and calm herself down. One evening, she tells her best friend that she can’t handle it anymore. Her friend tells her about a psychiatrist, and as a last resort, Lisa goes to see her. The psychiatrist first thinks it's depression. But then,

asking about Lisa’s childhood and family history, she notices something else and requests more tests. A few weeks later, Lisa is diagnosed with ADHD and depression.
