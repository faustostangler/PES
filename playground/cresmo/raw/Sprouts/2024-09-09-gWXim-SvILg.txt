One psychological explanation of human behavior argues that we often have no idea what we want, and hence just imitate others — welcome to mimetic theory. It's the idea that there are really only two sides to human desire. Physical Desires are our cravings for utility. Metaphysical Desires are our longing for identity. If you are hungry, you may want to eat anything to satisfy a physical need. Or you might wish

to eat out — in which case you may pick a place that reinforces your self-conception, your identity, and the person you wish to become. Rene Girard, the French philosopher who established the theory, argued that most of our desires are metaphysical and called them mimetic because we mimic them from the people around us. We imitate others, because we long for their recognition, hoping that we will eventually become a heightened being that will be remembered — ideally forever.
