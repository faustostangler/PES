The Kuuk Thaayorre, an aboriginal tribe, does not use left or right in their language. Instead, they rely on cardinal directions: North, East, South, and West. They might say “Pass me the salt on the South-west of the pepper”. As a result, they have navigational skills similar to those of migratory birds. The languages we pick up and use, influence how we see the world, the perception of our surrounding, our sense of time, our memories, our behavior and even our sense of self.

Languages are often associated with culture, and with the context in which it was learned. Those who speak various languages access different parts of themselves. One study on Spanish-English bilinguals showed that people exhibit different personality traits when switching their language. When speaking English, subjects were more agreeable, but when they switched to Spanish they were more neurotic. With about 7,000 languages all around us, nobody knows exactly how they affect our minds. But they certainly frame our thoughts and influence the way we look at the world.
