Can planets exist around a black hole? Black holes can outlive almost to anything in the universe. When dust and gas swirling in their accretion discs clump together, this could form planets, also called planets, up to 3000 times more massive than Earth. We already know that stars close to black holes can survive. Every 16 years, one star comes as close as 17 light hours to the black hole in the center of our galaxy. This speed the star up to 83% of the speed of light. There might be a safe zone calm enough for thousands of planets to exist around every supermassive black hole. Instead of relying on a sun for light and warmth, they would rely on the cosmic microwave background. The black holes in almost gravity bends at space so strongly that the long microwaves of the cosmic background are compressed into much shorter wavelengths boosting their energy. To someone standing on a planet, it would shine as visible light or even brighter, depending on how close it is to the black hole. The massive gravity doesn't only bend light, but time as well. Near the event horizon, a year could equal thousands of years on Earth. The sky above would be a warped halo of light bending around a perfect circle of darkness. Maybe a black hole is not an all-consuming abyss, but has a softer side too.
