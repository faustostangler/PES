Our moon is boring, let's steal the new one. How about the reddest object in the solar system, Amal Thea? From its host planet Jupiter, it shines as brightly as Venus does from Earth. Sadly, it's just a pile of rubble with a radius 20 times smaller than our moon, and it's four times less dense, so we'd have to say goodbye to our tides. Let's try Titan. It's 50% larger than our moon, with an atmosphere four times denser than Earth's, which reigns liquid methane. We could send probes to explore its polar lakes, where some scientists think microbes could have evolved to breathe in hydrogen and exhale methane. Ruffetides on Earth would be a small price to pay for alien neighbors. Life is less likely if we took fiery EO. With over 400 volcanoes ejecting plumes of sulfur, at least 300 kilometers high, EO could give Earth a donut shaped ring. This would fade quickly as its volcanic activity mostly comes from tidal interactions in Jupiter system, but while it lasted particles from EO's eruptions could give a spectacular auroras. Still, if it wasn't for our moon in its current orbit keeping us stable on our axis, and stopping us drifting into chaotic ice ages, we might not even be here at all. Maybe it's perfect just where it is.
