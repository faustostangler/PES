What are the worst infectious diseases? In joint fifth place, armilaria and eight. Both kill around 600,000 people per year followed by diurel diseases claiming 1.1 million lives. Third and second places go to tuberculosis and hepatitis viruses with each responsible for well over a million deaths. But the top infectious killers worldwide claiming around 2.5 million lives a year are lower respiratory infections. Living with these diseases also drains people's time and money. Malaria alone is responsible for around 500 million lost working days in Africa a year, which is a heavy burden for individuals and the economy. At the global level, sick days from tuberculosis add up to trillions of dollars a decade. But for the world's poorest, catastrophe can hit much faster. Over half of all tuberculosis sufferers spend over 20% of their income just to get treated. One infection can mean selling livestock, putting children out of school, or falling into debt. But we can break the cycle by investing in early detection and prevention. By lowering treatment costs and enabling people to live healthy lives, every dollar invested not only saves lives, but helps to give an economic boost so places once held back by disease can thrive.
