Let's turn up Earth's gravity. Even with a 10% increase, the first thing you notice is that blood gets pulled to your feet, making you dizzy. And for people with weak hearts, the extra strain [music] could be fatal. Around the world, full elevator cabins carry 10% more weight and begin falling. [music] Old balconies and suspension bridges collapse, too, while 12,000 satellites would be dragged into the atmosphere and catch fire. Anyone already in the sky is

[music] about to have a bad day. Parachutes will open at the wrong time, while high-altitude planes are suddenly overweight and can't stay airborne. Gravity also pulls the whole atmosphere down on top of us. At sea level, this means more oxygen to ignite forest fires, and even though you get more in every breath, the denser air is harder to breathe. For submarines at 500 m below sea level, >> [music] >> the extra pressure is like diving another 50 m down, making some implode.

Catastrophic earthquakes begin to ripple across the Earth as brittle fault lines in the half-molten sandwich of our planet are squeezed a little tighter >> [music] >> and slip. But at least the stronger gravity would put pressure on carbon minerals at shallower depths underground to give us a few more diamonds.
