Will fungi take over humanity? So far, most infectious fungi are not able to survive inside of us. This is due to our relatively high body temperature of about 37° C. Global warming, however, might change this. With rising temperatures, fungal diseases [music] appear more frequently all over our planet. For example, the previously

unknown and highly transmissive Canada leads to thousands of cases worldwide. About 1/3 of those infected eventually die, but higher temperatures are also allowing fungi to mutate more often. Scientists are already warning that as global warming worsens, we're opening Pandora's box. Inside, we might find new types of fungi that are resistant to heat and lead to more severe diseases. Furthermore, fungi share similarities with our own human cells, which makes it difficult to develop [music] new drugs or vaccines. Since serious fungal

diseases in humans are still relatively rare, doctors are mostly not trained to recognize such infections. So to avoid a scenario we all saw in the last of us, we should proactively search for preventive measures.
