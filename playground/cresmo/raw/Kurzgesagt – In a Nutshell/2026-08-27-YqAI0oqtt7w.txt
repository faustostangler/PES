What's actually is dust? Only about 1% of household dust comes from dead skin and hair. It's mostly a mix of soil, pollen, and other particles from outside, along with tiny textile fibers from inside your home. But, that's not all. 1 g can hold thousands of mites, microscopic spider relatives that feed on your skin, as well as hundreds of thousands of their fecal pellets. When

inhaled, their proteins can weaken your airways' protective barrier, causing one of the most common allergic reactions on the planet. The smallest particles are even worse. Below 2 and 1/2 micrometers, particles like soot can get deep into your lungs and cross into your bloodstream, contributing to millions of deaths every year. But, dust also helps make rain. >> [music] >> Every raindrop forms around a tiny particle, giving water vapor something to condense on. That's how 28 million

tons of Saharan dust replaces the phosphorus tropical rains wash from the Amazon soil every year. Without it, the rainforest would slowly starve. And there's one final secret ingredient. Every year, Earth gets showered with over 5,000 tons of comet and asteroid dust. So, the next time you dust, remember, for every pinch of regular household dust, there's a few nanograms of stardust.
