Let's take 1% of the richest countries money and see what we can do with it. 1% of what these 5 countries make in a year is around $495 billion. Stacked up in banknotes, it would wrap around the whole world. With less than 1% of our pot, we could vaccinate every child in Sub-Saharan Africa against malaria, saving hundreds of thousands of lives a year. With another tiny slice, we could cut global deaths of women and childbirth by over 50%. With around half of the money, we could provide clean water to the world, twice. And with the rest, we could build around 240 solar farms, each large enough to power a city. Even when countries give a fraction of this in aid, they invest in amazing things, from access to clean water to saving lives when disaster strikes. But in 2025, the 8 budgets of many rich countries were cut sometimes by over half. For the world's poorest, this stopped life-saving treatments and emergency supplies. And it will mean a less stable world for all of us with more conflicts and underfunded health systems, breeding antibiotic resistant pathogens projected to kill millions by 2050. But this future isn't inevitable. With a tiny fraction of their income, rich countries can still help to build a better world.
