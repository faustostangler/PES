This is your brain on caffeine. It's the world's most popular psychoactive drug, lifting fatigue and helping you focus on daily tasks. But how? Caffeine molecules are a similar shape to a denocene, which builds up while you're awake and tells your brain to sleep. By blocking a denocene receptors, caffeine keeps you alert. It also tightens blood vessels and speeds up your breathing by stimulating your respiratory center, all while promoting the release of neurotransmitters that boost your mood and improve attention. But these benefits stop paying out at higher doses. Drinking more will keep improving your reaction times and keep your wake, but at some point your accuracy stops getting better and starts to get worse. Although caffeine temporarily blocks a denocene receptors, a denocene continues to build up. Once caffeine's effect wears off, it bounces back, making you need more just to keep yourself awake. Over time, higher doses are more likely to make you anxious, sleep worse, and can even trigger issues like irregular heartbeats. Luckily, caffeine withdrawal is normally short and mild, so if it makes you anxious or just try tapering off or just cutting it out, for most people, low doses are the sweet spot.
