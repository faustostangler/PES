No one is safe if polio returns. This highly contagious virus spreads through human feces or contaminated food and water. It enters through your mouth infecting your throat and intestines where it might apply silently before slipping into your bloodstrip. Polio usually causes no symptoms, but in about 1% of cases it invades the nervous system and destroys motor neurons causing paralysis or even death when breathing muscles fail. What makes polio so dangerous is that it hides. Most people show zero or flu-like symptoms and its long incubation period of up to 35 days can make you infectious or weeks while unknowingly infecting others. In the 1980s, it paralyzed over 350,000 children each year across 125 countries. But we fought back. Crowdfunded vaccines in a global campaign eliminated two out of three wild polio strains. Paralysis cases dropped by 99.9% and the last wild strain is cornered in just two countries. But it doesn't take much for polio to make a comeback. When vaccination rates drop, weakened vaccine strains can circulate, mutate and in rare cases cause paralysis again. Polio has already been found in sewage samples in countries previously free of it and some have even seen fresh paralysis cases. That's why this fight isn't over.
