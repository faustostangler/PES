This village was struck by an invisible killer. The prime suspect was a lake. Centres before the crime happened, carbon dioxide slowly leaked from volcanic vents into the deepest layers below its surface. The gas remained dissolved because of the high pressure and the lack of mixing. This turned the lake into a geological time bomb. Undisturbed, the lake remained like this for years, but it probably took just a small tremor to trigger a limb-nick eruption. The trapped gas burst out in a fountain up to 120 meters high. The waters turned red as iron-rich deepwater searched to the surface. A huge cloud of carbon dioxide rolls over the surrounding vannis 25 kilometers away. Silent and heavier than air, it creeps on the ground. An invisible tsunami moving it up to 50 kilometers per hour, pushing away oxygen and suffocating anything that breathes air. This has only happened twice on record, but the same deadly setup exists in other deep volcanic lakes. To stop the killings before they happen, scientists are working on degassing the lakes and constantly monitoring the gas levels and tiny quakes. We can lower the risk, but we can't predict the exact moment when a sleeping lake might exhale a wave of quiet death.
