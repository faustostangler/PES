In some species, your sex is determined by temperature, and with Earth warming up, male to female ratios are already changing. The sex of bitted dragons is determined genetically, but high temperatures can override this, causing male embryos to become female. We're not sure if there's an evolutionary advantage to being female in a hot nest, or if it's just a quirky side effect of their sex determination system, and they're not the only species. This is also true for crocodiles, some geckos and sea turtles. On one island, female turtles outnumbered males 116 to 1. Scientists have worried that some populations may end up with no males at all, but there are some good news, larger spiders. Even in the Arctic, wolf spiders are benefiting from an earlier spring and having more time to mature. Both males and females grow larger, with females growing the most, enhancing their chances of producing more and bigger eggs. With Earth heating up, the ecosystem is shifting, while some species thrive some of force to adapt or face extinction.
