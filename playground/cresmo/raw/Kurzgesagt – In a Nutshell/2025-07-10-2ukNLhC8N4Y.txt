Are galaxies moving faster than the speed of light? According to Albert Einstein, nothing moves faster than the speed of light. But that's only true for objects moving through space. Our universe is expanding. It stretches just like dough in an oven. All the galaxies

inside this cosmic dough recede from each other as it expands. The farther apart they are, the faster they recede. And eventually, galaxies that are far enough away recede from us faster than the speed of light. This is true for about 95% of the galaxies in the universe. But we can still observe the light they emitted in the past. When the

light of a distant galaxy reaches us, it's traveled through the expanding universe for a long time. So the light itself stretches as well. By analyzing how much the light has stretched, we also know how far away the galaxy is. The more it's stretched, the farther away it is.
