Let's cook an Earthlike planet from scratch. First, swirl huge amounts of dust and gas around a newborn star. Turn on gravity and let the dust clump together into something called planetessimals. Gently bake it in starlight for a few million years, but be careful with the temperature. If it's too cold, frozen water, dust, and slow gas molecules form gas [music] giants just like Jupiter and Saturn. But don't

let it exceed 1,000° C either if you want something Earthlike to form. The amount of ingredients matters as well. Taking too much solid material would give you a planet denser than Earth. Asteroids, comets, and even smaller pebbles carry ice from the colder regions of the solar system. So, use those to deliver the water to your planet. Next, let your world cool down

for a few hundred million years. But not too fast, as timing is everything. Heavy elements like iron and nickel, still liquid, [music] should accumulate in the core before a solid crust forms the outside. Give it a personal touch by adding a few asteroid impacts to your pot. That way, your planet can have moons and a tilted axis. Be careful,

though. Even with tiny changes to the recipe, you might end up with rainstorms of molten glass and ice clouds.
