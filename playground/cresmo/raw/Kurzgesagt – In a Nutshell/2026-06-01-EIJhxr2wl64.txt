Which year of your life is the deadliest? Life gets dangerous from the moment you're born, problems during delivery, premature births, and the risk of infections, or make tiny humans very fragile. But where your born can change everything? In many high-income countries where risks are lower, around one in two hundred infants die in their first year. Each is a terrible loss, but in low-income countries like South Sudan, that number can climb as high as one in fourteen. And the dangers don't stop after the first year. Over 2.5 million children a year die before they turn five. This is mostly from preventable causes like infectious diseases, responsible for over half of child deaths in sub-Saharan Africa. Around the world, the risks then start to climb. A year is never as deadly again until your mid-60s. But the death rates do rise in adolescence and more sharply in low-income countries. As you age, risks from disease and even everyday activities stack, which means that the deadliest year for most is in your early ages. But for too many, the first year is the deadliest of all. We can change this though, investing in neonatal care and tackling preventable diseases will save millions and make those early years a lot less deadly.
