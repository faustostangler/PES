Eching has been crucial to survival for millions of years, even dinosaurs got itchy and it's still deeply rooted across many species from snakes to fish to you. It can be set off by mechanical stimuli like bug trawling on your skin or triggered by chemical stimuli after that bug bites you. In both cases, an alarm is sent to nerve cells in the spinal cord. The message is relayed to the brain which then sends a signal to your muscles to scratch. This releases the feel good hormone serotonin, making you feel good while you're protecting your body. But it can also become unbearable. Around 1 in 5 suffer from chronic itch at least once. It has a range of triggers from diseases and allergies to psychological causes. Your oversensitive nerves can make it feel like you're constantly being attacked by fire ends. And it's a side effect of some medicines that can lead people to lower their dose of life-saving drugs. There's no single cure for chronic itch, but medications and stress management can help us calm and overactive itch and bring it back under control.
