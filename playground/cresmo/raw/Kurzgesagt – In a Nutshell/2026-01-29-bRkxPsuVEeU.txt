There are zombie proteins in your brain. Every prion starts as a normal protein, folded into a neat 3D shape and quietly doing its job, but sometimes it can misfold and become a tiny zombie, recruiting other healthy proteins by bumping into them and forcing them to copy its twisted shape. This infectious chain reaction creates clumps of junk proteins that kill healthy cells and cause various diseases. Although prion diseases are rare, they are extremely hard to treat. To deactivate and kill prions, you need to break the bonds inside of them, for example, by exposing them to extreme conditions like high temperatures and for a long time. But because good proteins and bad prions are chemically the same, any treatment that targets bad prions would also hit good proteins as well as innocent healthy cells in the surrounding tissue. Yet as scary as they are, prions show us that information could be stored in shape alone. This could help us better understand other brain diseases where proteins misfold and spread just like prions like Alzheimer's or Parkinson's. So these zombie proteins might just be what reshapes the future of neuroscience.
