How does flu kill you? Influencer doesn't kill you like a bonnet. The virus hijacks your cells to replicate killing many of them in the process. For some, this damage and the immune system's counterattack make the airsacks in your lungs leaky or stop working. In others, it weakens your defenses so bacteria can flood in causing sepsis and even multiple organ failure. The flu still kills up to 600,000 people a year. Around one in five deaths happen in rich countries, but over 80% of flu kills are in lower income countries where access to vaccines or treatments like oxygen therapy are limited. Flu hits hardest where immune systems are already weakened. And for stressed health care systems, one outbreak can cancel surgeries, exhaust oxygen supplies and overwhelm medical staff. Faring poorer countries to handle flu emergencies and other pandemics costs around $15 billion a year, less than 0.02% of the five richest countries money, but eight cuts are putting even these funds and their own people at risk. To really cut flu deaths, we need new, more affordable vaccines and a surveillance network to detect outbreaks which could save hundreds of thousands of lives.
