Let's evolve, so you can fly. To get off the ground, you'll need a pair of wings with a wingspan of about 7 meters, but to actually flap those wings you need stronger petrol muscles and a keelbone to anchor them, or they'd rip off instantly. Still, the rest of your bones are too bulky. Bird bones are hollow, with air sacs connected directly to their lungs, so each downstroke pushes a fresh wave of oxygen directly through the system. We'll make yours the same, so the faster you flap, the more efficiently you breathe. Our next problem is F-Rin B3, a small molecule in your spinal cord that makes alternating limb movement, aka walking, natural for you by separating the limb nerve circuits. Bird's lost this molecule, and are now used to flapping both wings at the same time. After getting rid of it, you just need fuel. Flying takes more than twice the energy of running, so you'll need around four cheeseburgers worth of energy for every hour of flying. So other activities like doom-strolling will have to be put on pause. By logically speaking, you're not a human anymore. You're closer to an Argentavis, one of the largest birds to ever fly, with plain-sized wings. If evolution got this beast airborne, maybe someday it could get us airborne too.
