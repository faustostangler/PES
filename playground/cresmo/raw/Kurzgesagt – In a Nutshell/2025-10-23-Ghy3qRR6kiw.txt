Touchcreens. How do they work? Fine conductive layers of horizontal and vertical threads run through the glass of the display, producing an electric field at each intersection of wires. When a fingertip approaches the display, it distorts the electrical fields of the intersections near the point of touch. Measurements at the edges of the screen locate the exact position on this invisible grid, and the software acts accordingly by doing whatever we want it to do. In that way, a touchcreen can act

like thousands of buttons that detect multiple touches and movements along the screen. Although touchcreens changed our lives a lot in the 21st century, the technology for touchscreens had already been invented in the 1960s. It took a few more decades of research and optimization, a video game console, and of course, the smartphone to finally bring it into almost every person's life. It significantly reduced the distance between humans and machines, brought dating down to the movement of the thumb, and caused toddlers to swipe and pinch windows.
