Who owns the drinking water? If all earth's water were 100 litres, usable freshwater would be half a teaspoon. Most freshwater hides in groundwater and ice, with just 0.3% in rivers and lakes. One around 90% of this is publicly owned, with states claiming the water within their borders more than 260 rivers cross countries which can spark conflicts. The rest is from private supplies and gets even more complex. In some places, land owners own the water, while in others, the first to use it kept the rights. But companies usually own the infrastructure not the water itself, which is recognised as a human right. Yet, over 2.2 billion people lack safe water, contributing to hundreds of child deaths a day. In growing cities, many rely on small private providers. Trucks and water kiosks plug gaps, but their expensive water isn't always clean. Meanwhile, bottled water companies manage a tiny fraction, but can worsen scarcity by draining sources and even selling it back as sugary drinks. Global demand is rising fast and governments are struggling to pay the bills. But if private companies step in, they must think long-term, repairing leaks, recycling water and protecting the water cycle. Only then can we all share that teaspoon.
