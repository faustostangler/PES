We're at war with malaria. Malaria is spread by mosquitoes but caused by tiny parasites that hide in your liver, then burst out to invade and rupture your red blood cells. Humans were losing for tens of thousands of years, but in the 1940s we thought we'd cracked it. We sprayed DDT everywhere, wiping malaria out in many regions. But this was also toxic for us, and some mosquitoes even evolved resistance. Then we'd applied anti-malarial drugs and eventually insecticide treated nets to kill the enemy while we slid. These saved millions, but some mosquitoes changed their behaviour and started biting outdoors or during the day. Recently we hit back with vaccines that teach our immune system to spot the parasites early, but as malaria evolves ways to work around them, we're now combining different drugs and vaccines to hit it from several sites. Now, we also genetically modify mosquitoes to pass on a lethal gene and crash local populations of malaria carriers. But tech alone won't be enough. With resistance and rising global temperatures stalling decades of progress, we also need preventative care for kids and better funded healthcare systems. It's the only way to win this war.
