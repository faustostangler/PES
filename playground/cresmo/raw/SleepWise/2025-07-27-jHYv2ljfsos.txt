In the same way, humans and other primates are more like evolutionary cousins than one being descended directly from the other. The common ancestor we share with modern monkeys was likely a small tree dwelling primate that lived around 25 to 30 million years ago. Over time, different populations of its descendants adapted to different environments. Some became the various types of monkeys we know today, while others evolved into apes. And one specific branch of apes eventually evolved into early humans.

Fossils and genetic evidence show that humans are most closely related to chimpanzees and bonobos. But even these are not our ancestors. We share about 98 to 99% of our DNA with them. But that similarity points to a shared past, not a direct line. Human evolution is marked by gradual changes, walking upright, developing larger brains, and using tools.

These traits didn't suddenly appear. They developed over millions of years in response to shifting environments and survival needs. So, while humans and monkeys are connected through evolution, we didn't come from monkeys. We came from the same ancient roots. Understanding that distinction helps make sense of how all life is interconnected without confusing the direction of evolution.
