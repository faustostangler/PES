Curr, an ancient Mesopotamian mythology, represents the underworld or the land of the dead. It is one of the earliest recorded afterlife concepts in human history, dating back to the Samarians. Regardless of moral character, the dead were believed to live in a dark, dusty existence beneath the earth, feeding on clay and drinking murky water. The goddess, Ereshkigal ruled over Curr, assisted by her servants and gatekeepers, who controlled entry into the underworld. To reach it, a soul had a past through seven gates, leaving behind symbols of its earthly identity at each stage. Her reflex how the ancient Mesopotamians viewed death as a continuation of existence rather than an end. Offerings to the dead were made to ease their discomfort in Curr, showing the early human desire to maintain connection with ancestors even after death.
