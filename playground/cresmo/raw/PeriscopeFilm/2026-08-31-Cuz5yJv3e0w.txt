  This silent black-and-white home movie captures scenes from the 1933–1934 Chicago World’s Fair – officially known as the Century of Progress International Exposition – though which of the two years this is recorded is unconfirmed. Held along Chicago’s lakefront, primarily on Northerly Island, the fair was a grand celebration of science, technology, and innovation. The footage showcases a wide range of international pavilions and corporate exhibits, each highlighting advancements in modern industry and culture. One notable feature is the Skyride, a popular cable car attraction that offered visitors an aerial view of the fairgrounds and its architecture.

  The Sky Ride was a transporter bridge (with a design similar to an aerial tramway or gondola lift) designed by the bridge engineering firm Robinson & Steinman that ferried people across the lagoon, Burnham Harbor, in the center of the fair. It was located near Northerly Island, but was demolished after the Fair, having carried 4.5 million passengers. The Sky Ride had a 1,850-foot (560 m) span and two 628-foot (191 m) tall towers, making it the most prominent structure at the fair. Suspended from the span, 215 feet (66 m) above the ground, were rocket-shaped cars, each carrying 36 passengers. The ride was built in the span of six months prior to the fair's opening, by a consortium of five companies: Great Lakes Dredge and Dock Company, Inland Steel Company, John Roebling and Sons, Mississippi Valley Structural Steel and Otis Elevator at a cost of about $1 million. It was reported that the attraction had 2,616,389 riders in 1933 and a total of about 4.5 million during the course of the fair. It was demolished at the conclusion of the fair.
