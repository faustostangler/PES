  This short "Not So Dumb" (1930s) provides a playful behind-the-scenes look at Major Herbet M. Dawley's marionette puppetry. A humorous puppet character introduces the subject before the presentation demonstrates how individual strings and handheld controllers allow operators to make marionettes raise their arms, bow, turn their heads, and kneel. The film then shows a larger chorus of puppets controlled through a multiple controller worn by the operator, and invented by Dawley. He shows how the puppeteer's own movements contribute to the rhythm and lifelike motion of the performance.
  
  Major Herbert M. Dawley was a painter, sculptor, inventor and engineer for the Pierce-Arrow Motorcar Company, a photographer, theater and film producer/director, screenwriter, actor, puppeteer, author, and animator. In addition to directing over 400 plays, his work in motion pictures resulted in a series of silent era live action films for the Metropolitan Museum of Art and a series of religious films, along with many silhouette animation short subjects. He was an innovator of stop-motion model animation.
