  This silent reel of WWII footage appears to be a "rough cut" of a film made for the U.S. Army Air Forces. The film came from the collection of filmmaker Richard Lyford, and given the innovative editing style shown, it was likely directed and/or edited by him. (Lyford served with the First Motion Picture unit during WWII and later worked for Walt Disney. In 1950, he co-directed and edited The Titan: Story of Michelangelo, which won an Academy Award for documentary feature.)
  
  The footage focuses on an unidentified air group -- almost certainly the  344th Bomb group -- operating B-26 Martin Marauder bombers. Most of the film was likely shot in Tampa, Florida during training, but montage'd to look like it was shot in combat conditions. The film first shows a practice bombing campaign and then clashes against German fighters. The B-26 was built at two locations: Baltimore, Maryland, and Omaha, Nebraska and was first used in the Pacific Theater, Mediterranean Theater and in Western Europe. By the end of World War II, it had flown more than 110,000 sorties, dropped 150,000 tons (136,078 tons) of bombs and had been used in combat by British, Free French and South African forces in addition to US units. In 1945, when B-26 production was halted, 5,266 had been built. The B-26 started the war with a terrible reputation due to the loss of dozens of crewmembers in training accidents, yet it finished the war with the lowest combat loss ratio of any of the American bombers due to its high speed and maneuverability. 
  
  It's not clear precisely what unit is shown in the film, although several aircraft have VII on their nose, suggesting they might be part of the "VII Bombardment Group" or similar organization.
