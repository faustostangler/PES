  This film "The Blue Streak Linotype" (late 1940s) provides a detailed overview of Mergenthaler's Linotype Model 31 and Model 32 composing machines. It was produced by Caravel Films of New York. The Model 31 is equipped with one to four magazines and a gas-heated metal pot with microtherm temperature control, making it ideal for high-speed production. The Model 32, with additional auxiliary magazines, offered greater versatility and capacity. Key features include a one-turn shift for magazine changes, a sensitive and ergonomic keyboard, and various safety mechanisms that prevent errors and protect the machine. Designed for easy maintenance, allowing operators to perform adjustments and cleaning without leaving their seat. Additional production aids like a self-quoting device and cooling mechanisms further enhance efficiency. Overall, Linotype machines are praised for their durability, cost-effectiveness, and ability to deliver high-quality compositions with minimal downtime.
