  Dating to 1968, "The World of Piri Thomas" shows life in Spanish Harlem through the eyes and words of Puerto Rican-Cuban poet Piri Thomas, whose memoir "Down These Mean Streets" became a best seller in 1967. The film features Thomas reflecting on his life growing up in Harlem’s El Barrio, portraying a vivid account of poverty, violence, and struggle in a Puerto Rican community.
  In this part of the film, Thomas recounts the dangers of street life, where crime, drugs, and poverty claim many lives, while describing his own brief drug use and harrowing withdrawal. Despite these hardships, he emphasizes resilience, human dignity, and the importance of providing opportunities for future generations, calling for love, understanding, and racial harmony.
