  "Dial A-L-C-O-H-O-L" was a 1976 alcohol awareness series about young people working at a hotline for alcoholics. It shows their efforts to help callers who are struggling with alcoholism, highlighting the challenges and emotional toll of addiction. The film was produced for educational and awareness purposes, similar to other films about alcohol abuse.  
  
  This particular film "In the Beginning" centers on Pete and Liza, an engaged couple whose families clash over whether to serve alcohol at their wedding. Liza’s Baptist parents strictly forbid drinking, while Pete’s family, more culturally relaxed, sees alcohol as a normal part of celebration. Their disagreement over faith, social expectations, and personal values nearly ends the engagement. Interwoven scenes from an alcohol hotline reveal similar conflicts faced by others—teens pressured to drink, families torn by addiction, and misunderstandings about alcohol’s role in society. As Pete and Liza ultimately choose a small, private wedding free from family pressure, the film underscores the importance of personal conviction, open communication, and resisting social or cultural pressures surrounding drinking.
