  "Log Rolling The Sport of the Lumberjacks" , is a circa 1960 documentary film directed by Gordon W. Hedwig that explores the sport of log rolling, a traditional lumberjack activity.
  
  
  The movie examines the origins and evolution of log rolling, or burling, a sport that developed from the dangerous work of lumberjacks who navigated log jams on rivers using dynamite. What began as a risky job has become a competitive and entertaining sport, now governed by formal rules and organizations like the Rolio Association. Held in Escanaba, Michigan, the World's Championship Log Rolling Contest features skilled men and women performing balancing acts and tricks on spinning logs, from veteran lumberjacks to daring newcomers. The tournament includes semifinals, consolation matches, and championship rounds for both men and women. Jimmy Heron emerges as the men's world champion, while Mary Mallet wins the women's title. The event closes with humor, as the referee is tossed into the water by the athletes in celebration.
