  "Not the Giant...Nor the Dwarf" (1972) follows the journey of young heroin addict John Sabora and other residents of Gateway House, a therapeutic community in Chicago where addicts confront their behavior and rebuild their lives through mutual accountability. 
  The program at Gateway House focuses on helping recovering addicts face guilt and anger honestly rather than through violence or denial. During therapy, residents like Bonnie confront deep insecurities and guilt over past lies, while counselors stress that true recovery requires emotional honesty and responsibility. The discussion expands to the role of parents, who often enable addiction through misplaced kindness instead of setting firm boundaries. Gateway operates through state funding and daily community donations gathered by residents, reinforcing self-reliance. The story of Udell, a former addict, illustrates the struggle of relapse and redemption—after being trusted with new freedoms, he runs away and later returns remorseful. Through humiliation, accountability, and renewed discipline, the program offers him another chance, symbolizing the difficult but possible path toward genuine recovery.
