  "Pavlov's Experiment: The Conditioned Reflex" is a 1976 educational film produced by Coronet Films. 
  The film uses a live dog in a laboratory in a re-creation of the famous experiments of Russian scientist Ivan Pavlov; the experiments explore the concept of reflexes, focusing on the salivary reflex in dogs Reflexes are automatic responses we are born with, and by studying them in animals, we can gain insights into human behavior. Pavlov’s experiment demonstrated how a neutral stimulus, like the sound of a metronome, could become a trigger for salivation after being repeatedly paired with food—a process known as conditioned reflex. Using a model of a dog’s head and a device to measure saliva, the experiment showed that with enough repetition, the dog began to salivate at the metronome sound alone. Further trials revealed that the conditioned response became more specific to the exact tempo of the metronome used during conditioning. The experiment also explored extinction, showing that the conditioned reflex gradually disappeared when the metronome was no longer followed by food. Ultimately, the film illustrates how conditioning helps explain not only animal behavior but also how humans learn and adapt through similar mechanisms.
