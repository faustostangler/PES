  Dating to 1968, "The World of Piri Thomas" shows life in Spanish Harlem through the eyes and words of Puerto Rican-Cuban poet Piri Thomas, whose memoir "Down These Mean Streets" became a best seller in 1967. The film features Thomas reflecting on his life growing up in Harlem’s El Barrio, portraying a vivid account of poverty, violence, and struggle in a Puerto Rican community. He shares his multifaceted identity as a poet, ex-convict, painter, former gang leader, and writer, and recalls his experiences of fear, alienation, and survival on the streets. Thomas discusses the challenges faced by families trapped in poverty, the desperation of children running away, and the physical and emotional hardships he endured, including prison and personal violence. Amid this, he emphasizes resilience, hope, and the importance of education, creativity, and opportunity for future generations. Thomas calls for social justice, empathy, and a new era of love, friendship, and human dignity, urging society to rise above hatred and oppression and to lift all people from the struggles of marginalized communities.
