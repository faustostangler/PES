  This 1967 film "Columbia River: Economic Life Line of the Northwest" provides a geographical overview of the Columbia River, highlighting its historical significance, the challenges of its basin (flooding and drought), and the transformative impact of major dam projects like Grand Coulee and Bonneville on irrigation, hydroelectric power, industry, and commerce in the Pacific Northwest.
  
   0:00 Main title. The Columbia River starts in the Rocky Mountains, near the Continental Divide. It was first explored by   Lewis and Clark  , who followed the river to its mouth on the Pacific Ocean by 1805. 
   0:42 The river is fed by glacier streams and waterfalls in the Canadian Rockies. It officially begins at   Columbia Lake   between the Canadian Rockies and the Selkirk Mountains. Other streams join to form the largest river on the Pacific coast. 
   1:17  The river flows southwest through Washington. Its largest tributary is the   Snake River  , which was the route Lewis and Clark took to reach the Columbia. Other major tributaries mentioned are the   Kootenay   and   Clark Fork   rivers. 
   2:04 The river basin suffered from years of flooding and times of drought. Early farmers relied on rainfall, which was often insufficient or came at the wrong time, leading many to abandon their land and crops. The irony was that the huge river basin had to be "protected from its own river."
