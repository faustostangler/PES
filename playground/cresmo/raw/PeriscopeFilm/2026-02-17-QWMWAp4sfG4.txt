  Shot in 1938, this amateur shot home movie shows a glimpse of life and culture in Mobile, Alabama, including the city’s "Azalea Trail", an expansive network of azalea lined streets and public gardens spanning the city, and the annual Mardi Gras parade. While Mardi Gras is commonly attributed to New Orleans, Frenchman Nicholas Langlois began the tradition in Mobile in 1703 -- making this the city's 107th Mardi Gras parade. The parades feature elaborate floats, costumed guests, marching bands and traditions of throwing beads, moon pies and doubloons into the crowd. It grew into a significant cultural event and source of community identity for Mobile. The filmmaker captures the beauty of Mobile’s landscape and architecture comprised of colonial revival style. It portrays the style of the 1930’s as locals dress up in hand made costumes for the event.
