  This silent film (which almost certainly once had a soundtrack) shows USAF military history spanning three decades of U.S. Air Force air power, training, weaponry and x-plane aircraft at Edwards Air Force Base; based near Lancaster, California. Edwards was constructed in the 1930s as Muroc Field. Following the close of WW2, it was renamed after Capt. Glen Edwards. Edwards is the home of the Air Force Test Center, Air Force Test Pilot School and NASA’s Armstrong Flight Research Center. It is responsible for much of the USAF’s research and developmental programs for flight as well as testing and development of aerospace systems from concept to combat. The finale of the film captures a historic moment in the Space Shuttle program as the Reagans, and test pilots Henry Hartsfield and Ken Mattingly met up after the STS-4 flight in the summer of 1982. Pilots conduct spins and rolls, technicians perform tests on crafts and evaluations on protective gear and systems. It shows what the USAF was up to after WWII and into the Cold War. Military action was concentrated on producing weapons to combat Soviet might. The Valkyrie was a prototype version of the planned B-70 nuclear-armed deep penetration super sonic strategic bomber. North American designed it in the 1950’s; it’s first flight would be 1964 and it was retired by 1969. The XB-70 was to replace the B-52 Stratofortress and B-58 Hustler. It’s high speeds made it difficult to capture on radar screens making it a viable competitor against Soviet inceptor or fighter aircraft. ICBM’s were introduced in the late 1950’s rendering crewed nuclear bombers obsolete. The B-70 program was canceled in 1961. It includes images of the Avro Canada VZ-9 Avrocar manufactured by Avro Canada for a special secret US Military project.
