If you're in computer science, AI will be coming for your job. So, if I wanted to be AI proof, I'd be looking to become a forward deployed engineer. So, AI is pretty good at those repetitive coding, planning, and testing tasks. But, it struggles when the work is messy. It can't sit down with emotional customers.

It can't deal with annoying people when production goes down. And that gap is where forward deployed engineers live. This engineer works directly with users to turn unclear real-world problems into solutions. They can sit in a customer call, notice a broken process, and then build a solution to fix it. And so, if you want to explore this role, here's your three-part roadmap. First, get

technically dangerous. Learn about APIs, databases, and deployment. Also, practice debugging and use AI to move faster, but make sure you understand why things actually work. Two, solve problems without instruction. Go to a local business around campus and find out problems that they have. Then,

create a set of requirements and code out a solution. Forward deployed engineers create solutions before they're asked. Third, learn to ship and explain. Once you create projects, actually deploy them so people can see them. Go on GitHub and LinkedIn and explain what you built and why it matters. Because that's what you

actually do on the job. Hope this helps, and follow for more tech tips.
