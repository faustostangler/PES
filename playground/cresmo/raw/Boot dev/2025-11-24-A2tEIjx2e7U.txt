The biggest mistake I see computer science grads, boot camp grads, and self-taught developers make when they start looking for a job is that they stop getting better. Some people even completely stop learning so that they can focus on applying to jobs or whatever that means. Look, applying to jobs does not take eight hours a day. If you're spending that much time doing it, I'm honestly a bit worried for your mental health. Let's be realistic. You've got a life.

Maybe you have just 16 hours a week to invest in your future programming career. That's fine. Given those constraints, my rule of thumb would be to spend half that time, eight hours per week, on applications, networking, and resume polishing, things that are actually going to get you the job. The other half of the time, the other eight hours, I'd spend building your skills, writing new projects, doing coding challenges, and working on advanced courses. If you spend all your time on job search-related activities, your skills are going to decay, and ironically, the job search actually gets harder over time. Of course, if you

spend all your time just learning and building, nobody's ever going to find you. So, once you feel more job ready, take a more balanced approach. Split your time and keep your skills sharp while getting your name out there.
