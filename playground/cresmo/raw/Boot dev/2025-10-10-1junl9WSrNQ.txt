Say a company earns $100 in their first year of business and then in the second year they earn $200, then $400, then $800, then $1,600, doubling every year. This growth pattern is an example of a geometric sequence. Each number in the sequence is calculated by multiplying the previous number by some fixed number, which we usually refer to as R. And in this case, of course, that fixed multiplier is two. Let's take a look at another example.

Let's say that we start with the number three, first number in our sequence, and that r is 4. Well, the next number is 3 * 4, which is 12. Then we multiply by four again, getting 48. Then multiply by four again to get 192, and then again to get 768. Now, of course, there's an actual formula for this math that we're doing. A subn which just means the

number that we are trying to calculate the spot in the sequence that we care about is equal to a sub1 multiplied by r raised to the n minus one. a sub1 just refers to the first item in the sequence. r is that common ratio the multiplier and again n is just the index of the number that we're trying to find in the sequence. For example, let's say we're trying to find the seventh number in a sequence where the starting number is 100 and the common ratio or multiplier r is three. We can just plug that into our formula.

A sub7 the seventh number in the sequence is equal to 100 * 3 raised to the 7 - 1, which of course is just 6. And because 3 to the 6th = 729, 100 * 729 is just 72,900. So that's our seventh number. [Music]
