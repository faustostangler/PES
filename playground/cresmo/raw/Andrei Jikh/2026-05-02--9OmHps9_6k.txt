The United States actually imports more crude oil than it exports, and here's the math. We bring in about 6.3 million barrels a day and send out about 4.1 million. That makes us a net importer, a K consumer of 2.2 million barrels a day. You can't be a net importer of something and also be the world's supplier of it. That math just doesn't make sense. One of those numbers is the paper price. The other is the price oil actually costs in the real world if a country needs a barrel delivered to it today. The gap between those two numbers is about $35, which is the biggest spread ever recorded in history. Paper price of oil is what's called Brent Futures. That's sitting at around $100 a barrel.
