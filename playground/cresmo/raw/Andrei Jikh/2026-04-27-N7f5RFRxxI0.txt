The US 10 year yield is the interest rate that everything else in the American economy is priced off of. Mortgage rates, car loans, corporate borrowing, credit cards, they all move with that number. And there's a level at which the cost of servicing America's debt becomes a huge problem. And that is somewhere around 4.6 to 4.8%. We are not that far from that number right now. In an oil shock that drives inflation higher, makes it harder for the Fed to cut rates to bring that yield back down despite what Kevin Worsh may or may not do. Oil goes up, inflation goes up. The Fed can't cut, yields stay higher, go higher, so the cost of the debt goes up, the deficits get bigger, and the cycle feeds on itself. That's what economists call that death debt spiral.
