Memory price inflation comes for us all, and if you're not affected yet, just wait. I was building a new PC last month using some parts I had bought earlier this year. This 64GB Tcreate DDR5 memory kit cost $209 back then, and it's right there on the sticker. Today, this same exact thing from the same place costs $650. That's over three times as expensive.

And just in the past week, we found out Raspberry Pi's increasing their single board computer prices, Micron's killing the Crucial brand of RAM and storage devices completely, meaning there's now going to be one fewer consumer memory manufacturer, Samsung can't even buy RAM from themselves to build their own smartphones, and small vendors like Libre Computer and Mono are seeing RAM prices double, triple, or even worse, and they're not even buying the latest RAM tech. I think PC builders might be the first crowd that was directly impacted in such a visceral way. Just look at this insane graph from PC Parts Picker showing RAM prices going from like $30 to $120 for DDR4, or like $150 to $500 for 64GB of DDR5. But the impacts are only just starting to hit other markets. Almost every modern device has memory chips in it nowadays.

Like Libre Computer mentioned on Twitter, a single 4GB module of LPDDR4 memory costs $35 now. That's literally more expensive than every other component on one of their single board put together. You can't survive selling products at a loss, so once the current production batches are sold through, more prices are going to start going up. The smaller the company, the worse the price hit will be. Even Raspberry Pi, who I'm sure has a little more margin built in, is raising their board prices already. Cameras, gaming consoles, tablets, almost

anything that has memory is gonna get hit. I can't believe I'm saying this, but compared to the current market, Apple's insane memory price gouging is actually in line with the rest of the industry now. The reason all this is happening, of course, is chalked up to AI. And I have no clue if there's any price fixing going on, that's something the conspiracy theorists can debate in the comments. But the problem is there's only a few companies producing all the world's memory supply. And these companies all realize they can make billions more dollars making

RAM just for AI data centers and just forget about the rest of the market. So they're shutting down their consumer memory lines and devoting all production capacity to AI data centers. Even companies like GPU board manufacturers are getting shafted too. Nvidia's not giving memory to them along with their chips like they used to, basically saying, "Good luck, you're on your own for VRAM now!" And that's especially rich coming from Nvidia because they're profiting obscenely off of all this stuff.

Anyway, that's all bad enough, but some people see a silver lining here. I've seen some people saying, "Well, once the AI bubble bursts, at least we'll have a ton of cheap hardware flooding the market for, like, homelabs." And yes, in past decades, that might be one outcome. But the problem here is the RAM that they're making, a ton of it is either integrated into specialized GPUs that won't run on normal computers, or being fitted into special types of memory modules that also don't work on normal computers. That and the GPUs and servers being deployed now don't even run on normal power and cooling, they're part of these massive server systems that take a ton of effort to get running, and you wouldn't even be able to do that in like the most well-equipped homelabs.

All this to say, we might be hitting a weird era where the PC building hobby is gutted, SBCs get prohibitively expensive, and anyone who didn't stockpile parts earlier this year is pretty much out of luck. I mean, even Lenovo admits to stockpiling RAM, making this kind of like the toilet paper situation back in 2020, but for giant corporations. There's not enough supply, so the people who can't afford to get some will buy it all up, hoping to stave off the shortages that will probably now last a little longer, partly because of that stockpiling. I don't think it's completely outlandish to think some companies are going to start scavenging memory chips off other old systems for stock, especially if RAM prices keep going up like they have. It's either that or just stop making any products.

There are some echoes here to the global chip shortages that hit in 2021 and 2022, and that really shook up the market, especially for small companies. I hate to see it happening again, but somehow here we are a few years later, except this time it's the AI bubble to blame. Sorry for not having a positive note to end this video on, but I guess maybe now's a good time to dig into that pile of old projects that you never finished instead of buying something new this year. How long will this last? That's anybody's guess.

But I've already put off a few projects that I was going to do in early 2026, and I'm sure I'm not the only one. Until next time, I'm Jeff Geerling.
