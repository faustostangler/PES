"You will own nothing and be happy!" I've heard that message a lot. A month ago, I had a second video on self-hosting on this channel taken down. YouTube said it was 'harmful or dangerous content'. I appealed that, but my appeal was rejected.

Luckily, I have enough reach, and I was able to get a second chance. Most content creators aren't so lucky, and content about tools like Jellyfin and LibreElec get buried over time. But it's not just self-hosting. On my second channel, I posted a quick video about my frustrations buying a dishwasher–yeah... a dishwasher! I bought a Bosch

500 series because our old one broke and we needed one quick. All the online reviews for it were glowing and Consumer Reports had crowned it as the second best dishwasher out of all the ones that they tested. But they were reviewing the 2024 model. The 2025 model hid wash cycles behind an app and swapped push buttons for a frustrating capacitive touch panel. The app requires a Bosch account and you have to connect your dishwasher to Wi-Fi. That is dumb. And yes, I know there's

a hack for local access, but that only works after you connect it the first time to Bosch, giving them your data. It's insane. So insane, this recent XKCD kinda hurts being so painfully true and poignant. Self-hosting is forbidden apparently, and so is using the dishwasher I just paid for. Even 3D printer hardware, once a bastion of ownership and right to repair ideals, is starting to get locked down. And that brings me to games. I've been on a retro computing

kick lately, restoring my dad's first computer and some of the computers I grew up using. And all that old hardware is neat, but the thing that makes the hobby addicting is the software that runs on those things. Like, take Myst. My family played this game together for hours, solving all the puzzles. I have a lot of great memories attached to playing this game on my old Mac. And if I want, I can put the CD in an old Mac and play it again. I can even run it today in an

emulator, like using Infinite Mac. I've been imaging all the games I bought as a kid and putting them on this Raspberry Pi. That way I can play them whenever and however I want. I can do that because I bought the games. And I still buy games today. But not all games are built the same. More and more

companies are tying up games to idiotic DRM that requires an internet connection or periodic phoning home, and if that goes away, good luck playing anymore. People who build things should get paid for their work. I'm not at all about pirating software. But if you click on a button that says "Buy Now" or you go to a store and pick up a game in a box, you should be able to play it. I mean, is that not obvious?

And some games do have online components. And yeah, do I expect Bungie's multiplayer servers to stay up until the end of time? No. But do I expect for popular games some enterprising community members would step up and build their own self-hosted replacements? Yeah, if it's a popular enough game. But some games

nowadays aren't designed that way. They're designed to basically expire and be unplayable after just a couple years. Some people actually care not only about playing games but also preserving them as part of cultural or even individual history, just like I'm doing with all these old Mac games. I don't even care if they ship me their source code or anything, though companies like id Software go the extra mile and do that at least, I just care that the games are built in a way that I could still play it after the company stops caring about it. After all, I didn't click a button that said "Rent Now," I paid $60 and clicked a button that said "Buy."

Any game that turns into effectively a rental should be required to tell you it's not actually for sale. It's a subscription service with an expiration date. We have a tiny fighting chance to slow down this madness. There's a European Union initiative called Stop Killing Games, and I put a link in the description. If you care about gaming, preserving history, or just preserving the meaning of the word "buy," I'd encourage you to go there and sign the form if you live in the EU.

If you're in the UK, there's a separate form. I'll link to that one too. We're finally seeing a pullback from 'cloud everything', and it starts with rebels like us pushing self-hosting, consumer protections, and freedom. Will we be heard? Maybe 2025 we'll start the pendulum swinging the other direction. Until next time, I'm Jeff Geerling.
