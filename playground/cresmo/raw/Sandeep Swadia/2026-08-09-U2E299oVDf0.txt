The sharpest people I've worked with all share this one habit that looks like a waste of time. Jeff Bezos built one of the strangest meeting cultures in corporate America. At Amazon, senior meetings often did not start with someone presenting a deck because the executives would sit in the meeting room and read a written memo for about 15 minutes. Why do that? Because Bezos believed that PowerPoint can hide sloppy thinking, but a written paragraph has to connect one thought to the next one. Writing a narrative creates more boring work, but it also creates more clarity. Before a hard conversation, before a big decision, before anything that feels foggy, write three things. What do I know? What am I assuming? What is the next honest action? In most cases, the fog will lift.
