Hey, it's Maria Haines here, and I'm super excited to share with you that my weekly newsletter is back. I wanted to share with you the top three stories that people clicked on this week. First is this great post by Will Reynolds at Sierra Interactive, and he shares how his company built this way to use agentic skills and share them and improve them across the company. So, what Will does is he tests out a skill, and then he gives audio feedback on what he didn't like about it. Then they have an AI agent that converts that audio feedback into GitHub issues. Once a

human reviews the proposed fixes and the changes are implemented, then the skill is sent to their company-wide Claude so that everyone in the company who uses it can have this updated skill. Such a great idea. And speaking of skills, this next bit of research from Google is really, really exciting. They talk about Wiki skills. Now, this is not OKF. You

may have seen my videos on using the Open Knowledge format. Wiki skills is a way that uses the LLM Wiki format. It's a framework where skills can be stored and then improved upon, and agents can learn from whether or not the skill was useful and implement changes to improve upon the skills. And all of the versions, whether it was the improved or the old skill, are saved so that nothing is lost. Eventually, our agents will just be kind of like employees that you teach them the basics, and then they go off and improve and make things even better. And the number one story that

And what boost does is if you want to do something really complicated with Antigravity, it will separate it into different tasks that sub-agents can do, and then those agents go off and accomplish different parts of the task, and they independently verify the solutions for what they're trying to prove.
