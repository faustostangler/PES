  Can we leverage the power of AI to help manage chronic pain? 
  
  Introducing PainApp, an experimental app developed using the Google Gemini API that aims to do just that. 
  
  PainApp is built on the principle of neuroplasticity - the brain's ability to rewire itself. It guides the user through a series of interactive exercises designed to promote positive neurotransmitter production, followed by personalized visualization sessions generated via the Gemini API. Neurotransmitters such as dopamine, serotonin and oxytocin help our brains to create and strengthen new synapses and learn new pathways. 
  
  The app begins by asking users to describe their pain experience. This information is then used by Gemini to create tailored visualization scripts and mantras, helping users focus their attention on a pain-free state. By combining cognitive techniques with AI-driven personalization, PainApp aims to facilitate a shift in neural pathways associated with chronic pain.
  
  Thank you to my parents who volunteered to test this app out and appear in this video. My step mother Jen had 8/10 hip pain. By the end of the exercises her hip was pain free! My dad, Tony, has significant pain. We focused on his tailbone pain. While it did not completely go away, it was dramatically reduced. He asked, "What are you doing to me?" I told him it was Gemini who was helping!
  
  This app came to be after I had a realization that our brains are essentially a neural network - more specifically a sparse mixture of experts model - very similar to the breakthrough technology that is behind Gemini 1.5. I learned more about this when sharing my theories on the brain with Jeff Dean when he randomly sat next to me at lunch during the Google I/O developer conference. I did not know at the time that he was one of the authors on Google's paper on the sparse mixture of experts model. 
  
  I thought, "Well, if an LLM was not functioning optimally, how would we fix that?" The answer seemed clear to me - it could be retrained by repeatedly showing it examples of good, wanted behaviour. 
  
  This is what the app aims to do. If we visualize ourselves as pain free, and especially in the presence of good neurotransmitters we can teach our brains to create new, healthy, pain-free pathways.
  
  The app is not yet live. (I am not really a developer! I used Gemini, ChatGPT and Claude to help me program this.) However, who knows - perhaps one day PainApp will be available for everyone to use!
