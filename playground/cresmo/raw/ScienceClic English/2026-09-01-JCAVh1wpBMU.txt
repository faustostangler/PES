>> The speed of light is an absolute limit, which constrains the wildest dreams [music] of interstellar travel. In 1994, Mexican physicist Miguel Alcubierre proposed a solution halfway between physics and fiction, the warp drive. >> [music] >> So, how does this work? What are its limitations? Welcome back to Science Click. Today, Alcubierre's warp drive.

Special relativity teaches us that nothing can exceed the speed of light through space. This applies to all bodies. A round trip to Andromeda, for instance, necessarily takes over 5 million years, because that is the time it takes light to travel over the corresponding distance. Relativity also teaches us that the perceived duration of travel is shorter inside the spacecraft due to our motion, such that the journey may actually seem quick to us, only a few minutes if we are moving fast enough. But by the time we return to Earth, 5 million years will have inevitably passed there.

To find a solution, Alcubierre focused on general relativity. This model teaches us that space-time, the fabric of the universe, is flexible and can bend, explaining, among other things, the phenomenon of gravitation. His idea is the following. If we could deform space-time to create a bubble, might we be able to propel this bubble faster than light. This time, it wouldn't be the spacecraft moving, but the fabric of space itself, propelled by the curvature of the universe.

Its speed could thus be arbitrary, as fast as desired, and the spacecraft would be left surfing a superluminal wave. Indeed, nothing prevents space itself from moving faster than light. This is what happens, for instance, with the expansion of the universe. Relative to us, some very distant regions are receding faster than light because space expands more rapidly between them and us. Thus, nothing prevents us, at least in theory, from imagining a bubble of space moving faster than light.

Alcubierre's warp drive is essentially a bubble of space carrying all objects it encounters. Behind it, space would expand, pushing objects away. While in front of it, space would contract, attracting objects, and thus propelling itself forward. Inside the bubble, a stationary body would remain motionless without perceiving anything particular. No thrusters are needed, as it is space itself that is propelled.

The warp drive could therefore accelerate extremely rapidly without any acceleration being felt within the ship. Furthermore, space-time inside the bubble would be completely ordinary, flat, and identical to the outside, such that time would flow in the same way. Our journey would take exactly the same duration from our perspective as for the people left on Earth. we could in such a case connect Earth to Andromeda in a few seconds and our clock [music] on board would remain perfectly synchronized with that of Earthlings. To get a clearer picture, let's see what happens in a space-time diagram.

Alcubierre's model decomposes space-time into successive slices, each representing a different instant. Each slice is a perfectly flat space with no curvature and the instants are stacked one onto the other.

But while space is perfectly flat, time is curved. Inside the warp drive, its direction is tilted from one slice to the next. Inside the bubble, the future is no longer in its natural direction. It is tilted forward, pulling all objects with it. Bodies remain stationary relative to the natural flow of time, which carries them along these trajectories.

>> [music] >> Warning, Alcubierre's warp drive is often portrayed by this image, which might suggest that space is curved. In reality, as we have seen, each slice is perfectly flat. It is only the direction of time which is bent. This diagram, taken from the original 1994 article, is actually a schematic representation of an abstract quantity, the expansion factor, which measures how much trajectories diverge from each other over time. The expansion is positive at the rear and negative at the front.

>> What would it then take to create such a structure? How can we tilt the direction of time and create a warp drive bubble in order to move faster than light? The theory of general relativity tells us three things. Spacetime is flexible, it can bend. Its curvature the trajectories of objects.

And this curvature is directly caused by mass and energy. Around the earth for instance, it is the presence of our very massive planet that generates the curvature of spacetime and makes an apple fall. Thus, if we wish to precisely warp spacetime, the theory allows us to calculate where and in which quantities we would need to place matter in order to generate this curvature. In our case, the only curvature we need would be located at the surface of the bubble, which is where we would need to place matter. Mathematics allow us to determine the density across this sphere.

The matter would need to be more dense on the sides forming a sort of diffuse ring that encircles the direction of motion. But for all this to work, the theory also requires that this matter a very strange properties. And in particular, that its mass be negative. The mass of all known objects is positive. They attract each other.

But to create a warp drive, one would need a form of exotic matter with negative mass, which would repel objects in order to propel the bubble. As of today, everything seems to indicate that negative mass doesn't exist. Some quantum phenomena come close at the particle scale, but nothing quite sufficient to build a warp drive. To obtain a 50-m bubble moving at the speed of light would already require exotic matter with a mass four times that of Jupiter. Warning, it is sometimes said that Alcubierre's warp drive uses its negative mass to expand space behind it and that it would need positive mass in front of it to contract space like ordinary gravity.

In reality, this is false. The energy distribution is negative everywhere and localized not at the front and back, but rather on the sides of the bubble all around it. There are, in fact, other examples of warp drives that don't expand space at all and simply make it slide around them, but which also require a shell of negative matter.

Moreover, the warp drive presents many other problems. For instance, a restriction coming from quantum physics actually requires that its boundary be extremely thin, much thinner than an atom, and that it contain more exotic matter than the entire mass of the observable universe. Faster than light travel could also cause paradoxes and time loops. An individual could, for example, travel instantly to another galaxy and then return to Earth in the past before their own birth. Or, from inside the bubble, the spaceship would be unable to control it and would therefore be incapable of decelerating on its own.

Other methods have been proposed, such as the Krasnikov tube, which would allow us to return to Earth a few moments after our departure following a long journey. But at present, no solution seems realistic. Faster than light travel remains a theoretical curiosity, which allows us to question our models and the limitations of our world, but which belongs more to fiction than reality. Nonetheless, we can still have fun and imagine what we would see if we were ever able to build a warp drive. Indeed, mathematics allows us to trace the path of light rays from distant stars, which are carried along and deflected by the movement of the warp drive, thereby causing strange optical distortions.
