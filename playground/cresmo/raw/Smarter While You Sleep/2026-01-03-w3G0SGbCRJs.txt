bent shape signals vulnerability. An expanded stance signals dominance. These patterns are deeply wired, so even the simplest symbols activate them. Emotionally, this reveals how fast judgment really is. You feel emotion before analysis begins. A stick figure

slumped forward feels sad without explanation. One leaning forward with raised arms feels aggressive or excited. Your brain does not ask whether the figure is real. It responds as if it represents a mind. What makes this unsettling is how easily emotion is projected. If a few lines can trigger

empathy, then perception is doing far more work than reality itself. You are not reading the drawing. You are reading yourself into it. The brain supplies a story, a motive, a feeling, and treats it as meaningful. This explains why emojis work, why logos feel friendly or hostile, and why abstract art can feel emotional. What you perceive is not the world itself, but your brain's best guess of what the world should be like right now. Your senses collect fragments, light, vibration, pressure, chemicals. These signals are incomplete and noisy. The brain fills in gaps, smooths edges, stabilizes motion, and removes contradictions. Colors are assigned.

Depth is inferred. Objects feel solid. Continuity is invented. All of this happens before awareness arrives. By the time you feel present, the simulation is already running. What makes this

emotionally unsettling is how convincing it is. You feel like an observer of reality when in truth you are immersed in an internal reconstruction. Hallucinations, optical illusions, and perceptual errors reveal this system briefly, showing that perception can change without reality changing at all. The simulation adapts faster than truth. This model is not designed for accuracy.

It is designed for survival. Your brain favors usefulness over correctness. It exaggerates threats, simplifies complexity, and prioritizes patterns that helped ancestors stay alive. Comfort, fear, beauty, and meaning are all layered onto reality by neural interpretation. The deeper discomfort comes from realizing there is no direct access point. You cannot step outside

the simulation to check it against reality. Everything you know, feel or believe passes through the same internal filter. Even doubt itself is simulated. This reframes certainty. You are not standing in the world looking out. You

are inside a prediction engine, constantly updating a story that feels continuous enough to live inside. Reality exists, but you never meet it raw. You meet a version shaped for you, tuned to your biology, history, and expectations. Your experience feels solid because it must. If the simulation failed, so would your ability to function.
