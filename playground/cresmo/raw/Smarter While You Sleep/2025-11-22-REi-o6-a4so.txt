resentment, passive aggressive behavior, or even emotional detachment. What makes this truth so disturbing is how deeply unresolved anger can impact a person's life. It doesn't just disappear. It seeps into interactions, creating distance between people and making them less capable of truly connecting. Individuals may find themselves snapping at others over trivial matters, unable to trace the outbursts back to the anger they've been holding on to.

Alternatively, some may withdraw completely, avoiding confrontation and allowing the anger to fester in isolation. This silent internal battle can lead to anxiety, depression, and a breakdown in communication, making it difficult to resolve the underlying issues. The consequences of unresolved anger are farreaching. It doesn't just hurt the person holding on to it. It damages relationships, distorts perspectives, and prevents emotional healing. What's truly alarming is how

easy it is to overlook this deep, unresolved anger, rationalizing it as just a part of life or nothing serious. But in reality, the longer anger is left unchecked, the more it controls our lives, pushing us further from resolution, peace, and authentic connection. Validation from others is often sought over self-acceptance. One of the most troubling aspects of human behavior is the intense need for external validation often placed above the far more powerful and lasting pursuit of self-acceptance. In a world where social media approval and recognition from others are constantly emphasized, many people find themselves seeking validation from external sources rather than cultivating an inner sense of worth. Whether it's through likes,

compliments, or praise, the desire to be seen, acknowledged, and admired can dominate our actions and decisions. The problem is that this validation is fleeting, superficial, and often entirely dependent on others opinions, leaving individuals trapped in a cycle of constantly seeking approval to feel good about themselves. What makes this truth particularly disturbing is how much it distorts self-perception. People become so consumed with seeking others validation that they lose sight of their own values, desires, and self-worth. They shape their actions, appearance, and even personality around what they think will gain approval rather than embracing who they truly are. The longer

this external validation is sought, the further one drifts from authentic self-acceptance, creating a sense of emptiness that can never be truly filled by others opinions. The emotional toll is profound. This constant need for external affirmation erodess self-esteem as people begin to measure their worth against the fluctuating opinions of others. When that validation is absent, feelings of inadequacy and insecurity take root, leading to anxiety, depression, and even a distorted sense of self. Ultimately, when validation from others is prioritized over self-acceptance, people are left with an unstable foundation of self-worth, one that can be shattered in an instant by rejection or criticism. True peace and

happiness come not from the approval of others, but from the quiet confidence that comes with fully embracing and accepting oneself.
