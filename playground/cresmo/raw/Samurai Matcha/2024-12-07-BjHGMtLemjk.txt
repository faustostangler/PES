Welcome back to my channel! In this world, there are magical items that serve more than one purpose. In other words, if you have just “one”, you don’t need anything else. Today, I want to introduce 10 of my favorite magic helpers of minimalism that I personally use. Let's dive in! ① Tenugui (Japanese Hand Towel)

A tenugui is like a magical towel with at least 12 different uses! It's a traditional Japanese cotton cloth, super thin with lots of cute designs. Here are just some of the ways you can use it: 1. Face towel 2. Handkerchief 3. Bath towel

Those are easy to imagine, right? But it doesn’t stop there: 4. Wet towel (Oshibori) 5. Tablecloth 6. Wall hanging (Tapestry)

It can even become part of your wardrobe! 7. Bandana 8. Scarf 9. Mask And it’s great for wrapping things too: 10. Gift wrap 11. Bottle holder

But here’s the coolest part: ever wonder why the edges aren’t sewn? Some people on Amazon even left negative reviews, saying, “Why is this falling apart? I thought it was made in Japan!”

It’s intentional! You’re supposed to be able to tear it by hand. Back in the day, people would use it as an emergency string—like to fix their sandals. Pretty neat, right? I always carry one in my little pouch. Having a tenugui with me

feels like carrying a Swiss Army knife! Lightweight and so useful. ② Small Bag Let me show you what’s inside my everyday bag. It’s tiny, but it has two compartments—one for small stuff and one for cash/cards. So basically, this is my “wallet” and “pouch” all in one!

Here’s what I usually carry: - Cards, cash - Earplugs, pen, passport (when traveling) - Mini envelopes for cash gifts - My new obsession: nose mint (makes your nose tingle, super refreshing!) - Earphones, SIM pin, and of course… a tenugui! What’s great about this bag is that it’s also a “security pouch”. I can wear it around my neck or waist to keep my stuff safe. So, it’s a wallet, a pouch, and a security bag—three-in-one magic!

③ White Box This white box in my room serves three purposes: 1. Storage box 2. Bookshelf 3. Table Each box holds something different—one has 1, another holds books, and the last one has essentials. You might think, “That’s a pretty small storage box.” But that’s

the point. Limited space means fewer unnecessary things! So I keep it small. When there’s too much space, we tend to fill it with stuff This is one of the tips for living more minimally. ④ MOFT Case This is the laptop case I carry with me all the time. But it's not just any laptop case—it doubles as a laptop stand!

Ever find yourself working at a café with a table that's too low, and your neck starts hurting? This case solves that problem. You can adjust it to two different heights for better posture. I’m actually writing this script on my laptop using this case right now. Thanks to it, I can work comfortably from anywhere!

⑤Aroma oils I love using aroma oils to make my room smell amazing! Also I love using them as a fabric softener. I'm staying at my parents' place temporarily, so I haven’t made any recently, but I used to use original liquid with citric acid and aroma oils instead of fabric softener. ・ ・ ・ ・ Citric acid 30g Water 200g 50 drops of essential oil ・ ・ ・ ・ It’s very easy to make. I used that special liquid for about 50g per washing.

It’s amazing! Adding citric acid made the laundry fluffy and thanks to the aroma oils, the room filled with a nice aroma after doing laundry. ⑥ Hakozen (Traditional Japanese Box Table) The “hakozen” is an old-school table that people in Japan used in the past. Yes, it’s a table! Back then, people didn’t eat together at one big table like today. Instead,

everyone had their own small, individual table. And the coolest part? It also works as “storage”. Once you’re done eating, you wash your dishes and—“pop”—open the top lid of the hakozen to store them inside. It’s a table and a personal dish cabinet in one. Old Japanese people are pretty

smart, right? Honestly, if I could, I’d love to time-travel and live back in those days. ⑦ Aladdin Pants Sorry, I don’t know the official name, so I call them “Aladdin pants” I’ve spent years trying to find the perfect pants for Japan’s hot and humid summer—and I’ve finally found them. These pants are lightweight, super breathable, and don’t get sticky even when you sweat. They’re perfect for summer!

But what makes them really special is that they can double as pants for wearing with a **kimono**. When you wear a kimono, especially in hot weather, guys usually lift the hem of the kimono and tuck it into the obi (belt) to stay cool. This is called “shirippashori” The shape and length of these Aladdin pants are “just right” for this look. If I wore my regular pants, it would look awkward, but these fit perfectly under a kimono. So yeah—these pants work both as regular summer pants and as kimono pants. How awesome is that?

⑧ Tatami Tatami is a traditional Japanese mat, and it's become an essential part of my minimalist lifestyle. Over the years, I’ve discovered that tatami has at least “10 uses” ! Let me explain. First, here's what Heinrich Schliemann, a German archaeologist, said about it: "The tatami serves as a chair, sofa, table, bed, and mattress." That’s already “5 roles” right there. But I’ve discovered even more! Let’s dive into the others:

6. Yoga Mat It might be a bit slippery, but it’s softer than regular yoga mats, and I love using it for my practice. 7. Air Purifier Since tatami is made from **igusa grass**, it absorbs unpleasant odors and harmful substances, keeping the room fresh. 8. Soundproofing Mat Because of its cushioning,

tatami absorbs sound, meaning even if you jump around, it won’t disturb anyone downstairs. 9. Aromatherapy Forest Bath Tatami contains “phytoncide”, a component that gives off a natural aroma, similar to being in a forest. Inhaling it brings the same relaxation as a forest bath! 10. Shield Yes, a “shield”. Sounds wild,

right? But if you look at ancient Japanese scrolls, you’ll find illustrations of people using tatami as a protective shield during battles. So, if you ever need try it out. ⑨ Bags Of course, a bag is a bag—duh. But did you know a bag can also act as storage for other bags?

If you line up all your bags, they can take up quite a bit of space, right? But if you **store smaller bags inside larger ones**, everything becomes way more compact! It’s bag-in-bag magic. I think even Marie Kondo mentioned this method before. Give it a try—it’s surprisingly satisfying!

⑩ Drawers Finally, here’s a fun way to use drawers that you might not have thought of! This is my IKEA bamboo desk. It’s simple and practical, but it has one flaw—it’s a bit small. When I set my laptop on it, there’s almost no room left for anything else. That’s when inspiration struck: “why not use the drawer as extra space?”

By opening the drawer, I created a little armrest”! (Yes, I’m serious.) So that I can work comfortably. And hey, it works like a charm! So, we’ve gone through my 10 my favorite items—did any of them catch your eye?

At the end of the day, what’s really important in a simple life is enjoying it all. Instead of thinking, “Ugh, having fewer things is inconvenient,” try shifting your mindset to, “I found a new way to use this! Yay!” That childlike excitement makes all the difference. So, what new discovery will you make today? Let’s enjoy another fun day living the simple life!
