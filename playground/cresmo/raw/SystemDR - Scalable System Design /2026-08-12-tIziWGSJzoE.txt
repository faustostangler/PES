Stratified sampling, >> [music] >> splitting your analysis by a known relevant variable like country or device type, typically buys you a modest gain in the range of [music] 10 to 20%. CUPID, using a single well-chosen pre-period covariate, commonly gets you 30 to [music] 50%, cutting the traffic or time you need roughly in half in a good case. >> [music] >> And CUPAC, sometimes called regression adjustment, layers a machine learned model of the outcome trained on pre-experiment data on top of the same idea, pushing the reduction further still into [music] the 50 to 60% range in strong cases.

Three things worth remembering here. CUPID earns its keep on metrics with real pre-period correlation, revenue, engagement, anything with persistent user-level structure. >> [music] >> It's fear mode is quiet, not loud. Weak covariate correlation collapses data toward zero and gives you negligible benefit [music] with nothing in your pipeline flagging that it happened, which is exactly why you check that correlation before trusting the adjustment. And stratification only helps if your strata are actually defined on variables that predict the outcome. Stratifying by something
