Let's start with why this can't be an afterthought. Industry adoption surveys consistently find that around 65% of enterprise ML teams cite a lack of interpretability as a top barrier to scaling model deployment into regulated or customer-facing workflows. That's not a rounding error. That's a majority of teams getting stuck at exactly this point. Three forces are driving that number.

First, regulatory pressure. The EU AI Act and GDPR Article 22 both require meaningful information about the logic behind automated decisions that significantly affect individuals. That's a practical requirement. It pushes teams toward explaining individual decisions, not just publishing a model card. Second, trust and adoption.

Clinicians, loan officers, and end users adopt a system far more readily when they can audit and question its individual decisions. A model that can't explain itself gets quietly overridden, even when it's more accurate than the human. And third, debugging and bias detection. Local explanations surface spurious correlations, data leakage, and disparate impact long before those problems show up in aggregate accuracy metrics. Because accuracy metrics average across an entire population and can hide exactly the failure you most need to catch.

So, explainability isn't a compliance checkbox bolted on at the end. It's a design requirement from day one.
