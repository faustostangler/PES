Let's [music] ground all of this in a real named example, instead of an abstract comparison. Spotify's engineering [music] team has spoken publicly at conferences and in blog posts about consolidating its core analytics platform on GCP, built around BigQuery for warehousing and Dataflow for pipeline processing at the scale of a large, globally distributed consumer product. Why [music] bet on a single vendor at that scale? Consolidating warehouse, pipeline, and BI [music] onto one cloud reduced the number of distinct operational systems the platform [music] team had to run, monitor, and staff for. That's a real organizational cost being managed, [music] not just a technical one.

BigQuery's serverless model removed [music] cluster capacity planning as an ongoing task entirely. A deliberate trade [music] of some cost predictability for meaningfully less day-to-day operational toil. And [music] here's a pattern worth copying regardless of which cloud you're on. Spotify built Scio, a Scala over Apache Beam, and contributed it back to the open source ecosystem. [music] Because Beam pipelines can in principle run on multiple runners, Dataflow, Flink, [music] Spark, this kept their actual pipeline logic more portable, even while their operational choice was GCP first.

>> [music] >> You don't have to choose between single cloud simplicity and portability. >> [music] >> Writing your logic against open portable APIs captures most of the benefit of both.
