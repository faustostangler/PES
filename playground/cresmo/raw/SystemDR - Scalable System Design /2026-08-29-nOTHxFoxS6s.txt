Here's the mental model before any code. Every conversation in this system passes through three stacked layers, not one. Layer one is the conversation engine. It's multimodal, so it's handling text, analyzing images, and extracting from documents. And it streams its responses so the experience feels instant to the person on the other end. Layer two is

monitoring and analytics: response times, token consumption, satisfaction scores, and security incidents, all tracked continuously, not sampled occasionally or checked after the fact. And layer three is performance and compliance. This is where model selection, response caching, and resource allocation get decided in real time, and where GDPR, CCPA, and SOX requirements actually get enforced rather than just documented in a wiki somewhere. The key insight holding these three layers together is separation of concerns done right. Streaming keeps the user experience instant, while security scanning, audit logging, and compliance checks all run alongside it in the background, never blocking it, never skipped, never treated as optional under load. That separation is what lets you

have both speed and safety, instead of trading one for the other, which is the trap most naive implementations fall into. Hold this three-layer picture in your head through the rest of the lesson. Everything else is really just filling in the mechanics of these three boxes.
