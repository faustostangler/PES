Let's start with a question. [music] How much of your last ML project was actually spent labeling and cleaning data versus [music] training and tuning models? There's a number that gets cited constantly in this industry. Somewhere around 80% of applied ML project time goes into preparing and labeling data, not designing or training models. I want to be upfront [music] that this is a widely repeated rule of thumb from data science surveys, not one precise measurement. But every practitioner in

this room [music] recognizes the shape of it, even if the exact number varies project to project. So, why does this matter so much? Three reasons. First, label noise caps your ceiling permanently. No architecture change fixes a corrupted target. If your labels

are wrong, [music] your model doesn't just perform worse, it confidently learns the wrong thing, and no amount of regularization undoes [music] that. Second, annotation speed rarely matches how fast you want to iterate. A manual labeling pipeline that takes three weeks stalls a model development cycle [music] that wants to move in days. And third, the labels that matter most, medical findings, legal causes, low-resource languages, [music] are also the most expensive and hardest to scale, because they require real expertise, not just attention. Put those together and you get the core reframe of this whole talk. Data-centric

AI isn't about asking which [music] model should I use, it's about asking which examples does the model most need labeled next. That question is what the rest of this deck is about.
