Welcome back. Today, you're moving past agents that call one tool at a time into agents that coordinate several tools at once under real constraints. This is day 13, the halfway point of module two, and it's the day where it works on my machine stops being good enough. By the end of this lesson, you'll have a research agent that runs multiple tools in parallel, tracks its own spend, and recovers when something breaks. The same architectural patterns Netflix and Google run at a completely different scale. Let's get into it.

Quick orientation before we touch code. You're in module two, week two, production integration and monitoring, and this is day 13 of 30. Everything up through day 12 assumed a single tool call at a time. Your chat agent, your code analyzer, your recommendation engine. That assumption breaks the moment a task genuinely needs several tools working together, which is most real work. A research agent is the

clearest example. It needs a web search, a document parser, and a fact [music] checker, often at the same time. And it needs to stay within budget and stay safe while doing it. That's the problem we're solving today. And to be clear about scale, Netflix processes over 200 million interactions a day. Google

handles billions of queries. Your agent won't run at that volume, but it will use the exact same orchestration patterns those systems rely on. Learning the pattern now means it scales later without a rewrite.
