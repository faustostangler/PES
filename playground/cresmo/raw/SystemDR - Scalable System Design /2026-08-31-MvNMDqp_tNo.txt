Here's the mental model to hold before any code. Every single agent-to-agent interaction passes through the same five-step handshake with no exceptions and no shortcuts for trusted internal agents. [music] Step one, agent A requests communication with agent B. Step two, >> [music] >> mutual authentication happens using certificates. Both sides prove who they are, not just one. The same way TLS

requires the server to prove its identity, but here both parties prove theirs. Step three, they establish an encrypted channel with a unique session key generated just for this conversation, discarded once it ends. Step four, and this is the step most systems skip. The agents exchange capability proofs before any request is actually processed. So permission gets verified before work starts, not after something's already gone wrong.

>> [music] >> And step five, every single interaction gets logged and monitored for audit without exception, whether the request succeeds or gets rejected. The security payoff of ephemeral session keys is worth sitting with because each handshake generates its own disposable key. Even if an attacker somehow gets hold term credential, previous conversations stay unreadable. That's forward secrecy, and it's the difference between one compromised secret costing you one conversation versus costing you your entire message history going back months.
