Tor short for the onion router routes your traffic through three random relays scattered around the world run by volunteers. Each relay only knows the address of the previous and next relay in the chain. No single party, not even the tour network itself, can see both who you are and what you're doing. Tor protects against network administrators and your internet service provider. They see you're using tour, nothing else. It also protect against websites you visit.

They see the exit relays IP which is usually located in another country. Tor also protects you by making sure no single observer can see both who you are and where your traffic goes. Finally, it protect against surveillance by powerful adversaries. This is what journalists, activists, and whistleblowers use. On the other side, tour doesn't protects against exit relay snooping.

The last relay can see the content of unencrypted traffic. Always use HTTPS sites when using tor.
