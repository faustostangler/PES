When you scan a port, a complete TCP 3-way handshake is performed. Your computer sends a SYN packet, which is a synchronization request. The target responds with a synchronization acknowledgement (SYN-ACK), and your computer completes the process by sending an acknowledgement (ACK). The SYN scan only does the first two steps. Kim sends a SYN. The server

replies with SYN-ACK. And then Kim never sends the third packet. The connection is never completed. The SYN-ACK reply proves the port is open and something is listening which is all Kim needs to know. But because the handshake was never completed, no full session exists from the server's perspective. This make the SYN scan faster and harder to detect since

only two packets are sent per port and the log footprint is smaller or sometimes non-existent. However, it requires elevated privileges such as running the scan with root or pseudo because sending raw SYN packets is restricted for normal users.
