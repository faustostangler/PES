On the island of Flores in Indonesia, this is how thousands of people are now living makeshift tents lying on the ground. You can see for yourself. We're camping under a tree. So, we need more tarpaulin sheets, drinking water, food, children supplies. We can't have them sick.

Some 1,300 homes were damaged or destroyed by a 7.7 magnitude earthquake just off the northern coast of the island. Many were still sleeping when the earth began violently shaking around 6:00 in the morning Saturday.

For those that have a home to return to, it's a question of whether it's safe. >> At the moment, we don't dare go home just yet because the situation isn't stable. We still worry that there might be more tremors coming. >> The nation's disaster response agency has set up temporary camps, including makeshift hospitals. They say 18 health care facilities were damaged during the quake. It's here we're meeting some of

the victims who survived the worst of the disaster. >> When it shook, I felt I was spinning. I didn't have the chance to get up. Just as I was about to get up, rocks fell on me, on my head, on my legs. I had my two kids with me, and we were all buried under the rubble. We were then evacuated

by someone. >> The Indonesian government is now racing to respond. Overnight Sunday, the Indonesian Air Force flew in 50,000 aid packets to the hardest hit areas, but locals say the relief has been slow to arrive. >> Until now, not a single tent was given to us. We're very disappointed because the newcomers should also be given tents, but we haven't got any.
