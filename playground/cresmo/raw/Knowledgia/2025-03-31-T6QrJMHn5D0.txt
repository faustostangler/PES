Why was Ireland neutral in World War II? First, under Amen de Valera, neutrality reinforced Ireland's independence, as memories of British rule and the Irish War of Independence made full support for the Allies unpopular. Lacking military strength, Ireland feared invasion from either side. Despite neutrality, Dublin quietly aided the Allies by sharing weather reports, allowing defecting pilots to leave for Britain, and also repatriating downed German soldiers and preventing access spies from operating freely. Though officially neutral, Ireland's policies ultimately favored the Allied victory while avoiding direct conflict.
