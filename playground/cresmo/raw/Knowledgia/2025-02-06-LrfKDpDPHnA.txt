Why did Italy switch sides in World War II? First, during the war by 1943, Italy's military was overwhelmed. The aim to expand Italy's influence failed. The disastrous campaigns in Greece, North Africa, and the Eastern Front exposed weaknesses in its forces. When Allied forces landed in Sicily, public support for the government waned. In July 1943, King Victor Emanuel III dismissed Mussolini and formed a new government under Pietro Badolio. Secretly, Badolio negotiated an armistice with the Allies, announced in September 1943, Italy formally switched sides, joining the Allies. However, German forces occupied Northern Italy, leading to a brutal civil war between Italian partisans and soldiers loyal to the former government, backed by Germany.
