How did Russia conquer Siberia? First, largely driven by the fur trade, Russians adventureed more and more towards the east. Secondly, due to a power vacuum, the Uremac Timovayovitch led Kossaks backed by wealthy Russian merchants, initiated campaigns and defeated the canate of Sabir in 1581. Despite fierce resistance from indigenous peoples and harsh climates and the following years, Russian forces continued to push eastward, establishing more forts and settlements. Over the 1600s, Russia expanded through diplomacy, trade, and military conquest, exploiting internal divisions among Siberian tribes. The vast and sparsely populated land was rich in resources, which funded further expansion. By the late 1600s, Russian explorers reached the Pacific, completing most of the conquest.
