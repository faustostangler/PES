Why didn't France fight to the end in World War II? First, the German Blitzkrieg in May 1940 overwhelmed French defenses fast, bypassing the heavily fortified Maginot line. The rapidity and intensity of the German advance left the French military in disarray. Technically France could still fight, but after a large part of the army being defeated, politically the country was divided and demoralized. Memories of World War I's devastation made many leaders reluctant to prolong fighting. When Paris fell and government fled south, Prime Minister Beton, a World War I hero, argued for an armistice to save lives and preserve French sovereignty in some form. The surrender in June 22, 1940 led to the establishment of a puppet regime. Resistance persisted, but the defeat reflected strategic missteps, political fractures, and desire to avoid further destruction.
