Why is Luxembourg a country? The journey to becoming a country began in 963 AD, when Count Zieg III acquired a small fortress known as Luxembourg. In the 10th century, it emerged as a small but strategic territory under the House of Luxembourg. Over the centuries, its location made it a prize in European conflicts. After the Napoleonic Wars, the Congress of Vienna established Luxembourg as a grand duchy under the rule of the Dutch king, tied to the German Confederation. Its heavily fortified capital made it strategically significant, leading to disputes between France and Prussia. The 1867 Treaty of London granted Luxembourg full independence and neutrality. This ensured its survival as a small, neutral state. Since then, Luxembourg has remained a sovereign.
