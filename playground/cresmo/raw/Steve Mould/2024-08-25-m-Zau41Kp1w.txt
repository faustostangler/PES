A cast saw is able to cut through a cast, but it's not able to cut through human skin. Don't try this at home. We need to film it in slow motion. Straight away, you can see that the blade isn't in fact spinning. It's oscillating backwards and forwards. And looking at

the footage, it's only moving back and forth about 3 mm. Here's a clue as to why the blade can cut through a cast, but not through human skin. Here's an under-inflated balloon, and here's an over-inflated balloon. The cast saw can't pop the under-inflated balloon. But look what happens when I press it into the over-inflated balloon. It pops

easily. When you over-inflate a balloon, you're pushing the balloon to its elastic deformation limit. It can't stretch anymore after that. So, when the teeth of the blade touches the rubber, the rubber can't stretch and move underneath it. The under-inflated balloon has a lot of stretch still left in it. The rubber just moves and

stretches underneath the teeth. Human skin is like an under-inflated balloon. As the teeth bite into my skin, they proceed to just casually move the skin back and forth.
