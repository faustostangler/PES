In this video, I'm going to show you how to make a black flame. So, I start with a methanol flame. Then, I add a bit of salt water. I'm illuminating the scene with one of these old-fashioned street lights called a sodium street light. If you excite an atom in the right way, an electron will jump up to a higher orbital. And when it drops back down

again, it releases a photon of light. An atom of sodium emits a very specific color of photon when an electron drops down an orbital. But, the opposite also happens. If you can hit a sodium atom with a photon that has just the right energy, the electron will jump to a higher orbital, absorbing that photon. So, as well as having an emission spectrum, sodium also has an absorption spectrum. And with that simple fact, we

can explain the black flame. All the time in that flame, you've got a mixture of excited sodium and sodium in the ground state, which are really good at absorbing 590 nm photons, which is the exact photons that are being produced by the sodium street light. And of course, if the flame is absorbing photons, it's going to look dark.
