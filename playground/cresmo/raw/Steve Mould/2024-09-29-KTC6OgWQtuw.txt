This is the mechanism inside a grasshopper's leg that enables it to jump so high. So, here's a diagram of the back leg of a grasshopper. There are two tendons. The one shown in red extends the leg, so that's called the extensor tendon. And the blue one flexes the leg, so that's called the flexor tendon. The grasshopper needs something

like a bow, something that can store energy and release it quickly. Everything you can see here is exoskeleton, but the darker region is a different type of exoskeleton. It's much stiffer, much better for storing lots of elastic energy. So, in our model, we've added a spring to represent that specialized exoskeleton. So, here's the sequence. First, the flexor tendon pulls

the leg up, and it holds it there. When the extensor muscle contracts, it's going to cause that spring to contract. Then all the grasshopper has to do is release the flexor muscle, and all that energy gets delivered with a large force quickly. Like that.
