This is a Chinese magic mirror. It seems like a perfectly normal polished bronze mirror. But if you take a point source of light and you shine it on the mirror, the reflection you see on the wall is actually the image on the back of the mirror. If you look at the lines of the drawing, every line is made up of a dark edge and a light edge next to each other. So, imagine you've got a perfectly flat mirror. We're going to

put a little step in it. You can see that this part of the step where it starts to level out again causes the reflected rays to spread out. And so, that's a dark fringe. And then this part of the step causes the reflected rays to bunch up. So, here on the screen we should expect a bright fringe. When we

move the screen further away from the mirror, eventually these rays bunch up to the point where they reach their focal point. But that's not true for magic mirrors. The mirrored surface is slightly convex. The bunched-up rays unfocus entirely. So, now it doesn't matter where we place the screen, there will always be a light fringe next to a dark fringe.
