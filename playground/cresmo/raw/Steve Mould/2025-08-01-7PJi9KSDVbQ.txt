This microscope uses touch. It's called GelSight because at the end there's this gel pad. The gel is specially formulated so that when you push an object into it, the gel deforms so that it can get into all the tiny nooks and crannies of the object. Just behind the gel there's a camera pointing at it and a ring of six lights. What you see in the preview image on the screen is what the camera sees when only one of those lights is switched on. By illuminating the

deformed gel in this way, the human visual system can discern the shape of the object using cues about light and shade and shadows and highlights. We can actually gain information that we would never have had access to with a regular microscope. If you present the computer brain with six images, one taken with each of the six different light sources, you can use a clever algorithm to analyze those six images and come up with a 3D model of that object.
