A while back I made a video about why it's hard to bounce a ball under a table. It always wants to come back to you because of backspin. And I got a few comments along the lines of, "Well, that's cool and everything, but what's actually happening during that collision? How does backspin actually change the direction of the ball?" When a spinning ball hits a surface, it experiences friction. Friction always

acts in the opposite direction to the direction of motion. So, here where the surface of the ball is traveling to the right, it's going to experience friction pointing to the left. And when an object experiences a force to the left, its velocity increases in that direction. So, we see that the ball gains some horizontal velocity towards the left. But, because the force is being applied off-center relative to the center of mass of the ball, the ball also experiences a torque, a twisting force.

This torque is clockwise, which is opposite to the way the ball is spinning. So, we expect the angular momentum of the ball to decrease, which is what we see.
