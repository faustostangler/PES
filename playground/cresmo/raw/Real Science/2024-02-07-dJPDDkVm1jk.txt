A rare pink manta ray was recently spotted off the Great Barrier Reef, and at first it's almost too crazy to believe. But photo after photo shows that this is the real thing, and that this manta really is this pink. His name is Inspector Clouseau. He's an 11-ft male reef manta and is the only known pink manta in the world. At first, scientists thought Inspector Clouseau's color was the result of a skin infection or diet, sort of like how flamingos turn pink from eating little shrimp. But

researchers took a small skin biopsy from the manta showing this not to be the case. Instead, they think he has a condition called erythrism. Like albino and melanistic animals, somewhere in his genome is probably a mutation that makes his skin pigment turn an abnormal color, in this case pink. Other animals can have this too, but seeing it in a manta is truly extraordinary.
