This is a 19th century armored coat from India. Look closely at its protection and you'll see what looks like dragon-scaled armor. But those are not dragon scales, they are pangolin scales. Pangolins are small and strange mammals that get hunted by lions and hyenas. But when threatened, they curl up into an impenetrable armored ball. It's defensive secret lies in these scales. The overlapping scales are arranged in a precise hexagonal pattern. Each scale nestled at the center of three surrounding ones, overlapping like roof tiles. This means the pangolin's body is completely shielded with no weak points between the scales. Within each scale, there are also multiple structural layers. This creates a complex interwoven structure where cracks or stress can't move in a straight line, making it harder for them to spread between the layers. So when a predator bites a pangolin, the load gets shared across many scales, preventing puncture or penetration, making it stronger than chainmail at defending against blunt or piercing stabs.
