Why would I want water with an extra ingredient if I could just have pure water? You know what I mean? Why would I need a government organization to buy water without water? Let's unpack what the word chemical is doing in her sentence. Because the rest of this exchange is going to be a slow motion demolition of her. There are two things that are going on here. What she's saying and what she's implying. What she's saying is technically accurate. A chemical is just an added molecule, something that wasn't in the water originally. Fine. What she's implying is something else entirely. That a chemical is an unhealthy contaminant, something bad for you as opposed to just have pure water. Why would I want water with an extra ingredient if I could just have pure water? Those aren't the same claim. Her argument, however, needs them to feel like the same claim. Because otherwise, she has to actually prove that fluoride at regulated doses is harmful. And that is a much harder fight. This is the seed of what will eventually become a full blown equivocation.
