My experience with atheists is too full. They have a very reductive notion of what constitutes God, and they've often been hurt by someone who was religious, and that's less than with an animus. There it is, the classic Peterson false dichotomy. Atheists fall into exactly two categories. One, two stupid to understand God properly. Two, two emotionally damaged to think clearly. Notice what's missing. The possibility, the mere possibility, that someone could understand Christianity perfectly well and reject it for rational reasons. Nope, in Peterson's world, that category doesn't exist. To get technical, this is an example of a false dilemma fallacy combined with poisoning the well. The false dilemma, also called a false dichotomy, is when someone presents only two options when many more exist, and poisoning the well is when you present negative information about someone before they speak, attempting to discredit anything they might say. By preemptively declaring that all atheists either can't understand or are too hurt to think clearly, he's ensuring that any argument an atheist makes can be dismissed. It's like a doctor announcing that the next patient that comes in is either too stupid to describe their symptoms accurately or too hysterical to be trusted. All this, before you've even walked into the room.
